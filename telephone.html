Public telephone companies, carrier succession, switching, transport and host-and-remote architecture.

07 / PUBLIC TELEPHONE NETWORK

Two systems became one

Avalon and Newfoundland Telephone served the larger centres. The government, Canadian National Telecommunications, and Terra Nova served most rural and coastal communities. The systems merged in 1989.

SERVICE TERRITORIES

CNT primarily served the island's coastal regions. Newfoundland Telephone served the more populated island regions and Labrador outside Labrador City. Labrador Telephone Company served Labrador City.

LOCAL SWITCHING AND LONG-HAUL TRANSPORT

From manual boards to fibre

  1. 1921Central energyMetallic circuits and a multiple switchboard replaced the inherited St. John's plant.
  2. 1948Automatic dialFour-digit numbers, party letters, dial tones and same-line ring-back entered service in St. John's.
  3. 1959Microwave backboneCNT linked St. John's, Gander, Corner Brook, Cape Breton and later rural branches.
  4. 1974SP-1 electronic switchingCorner Brook received the first stored-program electronic toll switch in the Newfoundland Telephone network.
  5. 1979DMS-10 digitalSpringdale opened on April 25. Newfoundland Telephone installed its first DMS-10 at Nain that year.
  6. 1980 TO 1982DMS-100 hostsCorner Brook and St. John's received host switches. Torbay was served by a remote line module from the St. John's DMS-100.
  7. 1985 TO 1986Host and remoteGander's DMS-100 hosted a remote switching centre at Glovertown over digital radio.
  8. 1986Digital backboneThe 670 km Corner Brook to St. John's route used 15 microwave sites and fibre at both terminals.
  9. 1990 TO 1993Cellular and fibreCellular service started in 1990. The Sydney to St. John's fibre route reached the capital in 1993.

Open unresolved dates and source conflicts
  • The 1977 study dates the Corner Brook takeover and dial exchange to 1947. A later encyclopedia gives 1948.
  • The 1977 study says DDD began in nine east-coast communities. A 1971 newspaper account says eight exchanges.
  • CNT's mainland radio route is described as 12 channels in 1950 and six voice channels by 1952. The sources may describe separate construction stages.
  • The final Newfoundland step-by-step or crossbar exchange and its cutover date have not been located.
