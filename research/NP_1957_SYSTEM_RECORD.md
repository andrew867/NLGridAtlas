# 1957 System Record

## Transmission

### 101L

101L was constructed in 1957 as a 32.5 km, 66 kV link between Grand Falls Substation and Rattling Brook. The route followed the Trans-Canada Highway and Route 351. The later condition assessment identified 29.5 km of original construction remaining, with 236 single-pole structures, five two-pole structures and non-standard 2/0 ACSR conductor. Separate 2.2 km and 0.8 km sections dated to 1975 and 1989.

## Hydroelectric generation and civil works

### New Chelsea

The 2004 filing says New Chelsea entered service in January 1957 with 3.7 MW installed capacity, a construction cost just over $2.5 million and normal annual production of 15.4 GWh. The 2013 engineering report instead says the 3.70 MW plant was placed in service in 1956 and gives normal annual production of 16.3 GWh. The service year and stated normal production remain unresolved.

The intake between Seal Cove Pond and the penstock dates to original 1957 construction. The filing describes a concrete foundation, trash rack, head gate and timber building. It separately says the plant control panels, switchgear and protection systems dated to the original 1956 installation.

### Pitmans Pond intake

The Pitmans Pond intake dates to original 1957 construction. The record describes a concrete foundation, trash rack, head gate and timber building. The intake therefore predates the generating plant's 1959 commissioning.

Source: [2016 Capital Budget Application, physical PDF p. 211](http://www.pub.nl.ca/applications/2016/NP2016Capital/files/applic/NP-2016-Capital-Budget-Application-2015-06-23.pdf#page=211), SHA-256 `9032c57d9f433b245ebcf28fb72b206550bd1caa6002c3f02c4203f284b04489`.

### Snook's Arm and Venams Bight

The 2024 mini-hydro assessment says both plants were constructed in 1956 and commissioned in 1957 for First Maritime Mining Corporation to supply the Tilt Cove mine. Snook's Arm has one 0.56 MW horizontal Francis unit. Venams Bight has one 0.36 MW horizontal Francis unit. The Newfoundland and Labrador Power Commission purchased both plants in 1968.

The Snook's Arm watershed has seven dams, six timber crib dams and one concrete dam with an overflow spillway, gravity section and intake. The assessment dates all seven original dams to 1957. Its 939 m steel penstock mostly dates to 2006; a 24 m original woodstave section remained. A June 29, 2019 fire left the station inoperable.

The Venams Bight watershed has six dams. Its concrete-faced rockfill intake dam, concrete spillway and five timber crib dams are dated to 1961. Its original 681 m woodstave penstock remained in the 2024 assessment, and the plant had been out of service since 2011.

Hydro's 2007 response conflicts with the later assessment. It lists both plants in service in 1956 and explicitly says the 1957 Snook's Arm date is an error. A 2001 response says the original owners built the plants to supply the mine in 1957 and says reliable individual plant records are available only from 1978. The commissioning dates remain unresolved.

## Station equipment

### St. George's breaker evidence

The 2002 capital hearing identifies oil-filled breakers at St. George's and says they were moved from another company site and installed there in 1957. The written CA-22(b) response identifies one St. George's breaker as purchased in 1960. The records do not identify serial numbers or establish whether the testimony and written response describe the same equipment.

## Operations property

The 2013 depreciation schedule assigns $28,575 of original cost to the 1957 vintage of the Topsail Road Transformer Shop. The 2016 schedule carries the same 1957 amount under the later Topsail Road Transformer Storage label. This is an asset-vintage record, not proof that the entire facility was constructed in 1957.
