# Hydro operations radio and microwave record

Updated: 2026-11-11

## Evidence layers

The public record supports three separate layers. The atlas does not combine them into one network.

| Layer | Evidence state | Record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Conventional VHF network purchased in 1989 and assessed in 2001 | Historical, verified from the 2004 capital filing | 29 repeater sites, one non-redundant ATI switch at Gander, St. John's dispatch, telephone interconnects, four-wire site links, and field radios. |
| Trunked VHF replacement filed in 2004 | Proposed | A standards-based trunked system in the 170 MHz band. The first-revision summary specified 39 sites. |
| Provincial radio and vehicle-data project reported in 2026 | Reported | Provincial P25 radios plus Starlink terminals and Cradlepoint routers for selected fleet vehicles. The filing reports that approximately half of the equipment was installed in 2025 and carries the remainder into 2026. |

## Conventional network assessed in 2001

Hydro described the conventional VHF system as purchased in 1989. The network used a central ATI switch at Gander. The switch had no redundancy. Each repeater site had a site controller, a mobile transceiver, and a paging transmitter. The filing states that the switch and site controller were discontinued in 1991, and that the transmit and receive equipment was discontinued in 1996. The fleet had approximately 350 radios, split approximately 70 percent mobile and 30 percent portable. Source: 2004 Capital Budget Application, physical PDF pp. 193 to 195.

The 2001 radio distribution list contains 47 pagers, 249 mobile radios, 85 portable radios, and 33 base stations. Source: physical PDF p. 251.

The filed block diagram shows St. John's dispatch, the ATI switch at Gander, telephone interconnects, four-wire site links, and repeater controllers. Source: physical PDF p. 258.

## Microwave changes from 1999 to 2003

Hydro completed a west-coast microwave upgrade in 1999 and an east-coast microwave upgrade in 2001. The east-west interconnection was scheduled for completion in 2003. The filing states that the microwave work allowed approximately ten repeaters to move from NewTel sites to Hydro sites. Source: physical PDF p. 200.

The 2004 scope also included six additional repeaters for southern Labrador, Happy Valley-Goose Bay, and Granite Canal. The first-revision summary dated 2004-09-24 later specified 39 sites, ten more than the existing 29. It identified added coverage for Granite Canal, the Bay d'Espoir Highway, southern Labrador, Happy Valley-Goose Bay, and the Burin Peninsula. Sources: 2004 Capital Budget Application, physical PDF p. 200; Mobile Radio System Replacement Summary of Findings, first revision, PDF p. 1, report p. 11.

## 2004 replacement economics

The 2004 study compared Hydro-owned trunked and conventional replacements on a net-present-value basis.

| Alternative | Capital | Operating | Total |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Trunked | $5,700,000 | $5,127,896 | $10,827,896 |
| Conventional | $6,625,000 | $3,972,828 | $10,597,828 |

The difference is $230,068. The study describes the difference as approximately two percent. It states that Hydro's share would be $115,034 if Workplace Health, Safety and Compensation Commission paid 50 percent. Source: 2004 Capital Budget Application, physical PDF p. 202.

The filing selected the 170 MHz band because the study found that it gave the required coverage with the fewest sites. Source: physical PDF p. 201.

## Later radio and vehicle-data project

The 2027 Capital Budget Application states that Hydro cancelled the earlier satellite-phone and cellular scope. It selected radios on the provincial P25 system, plus Starlink terminals and Cradlepoint routers for selected fleet vehicles. The filing reports that approximately half of the equipment was installed in 2025 and that the remaining work continued into 2026. Source: 2027 Capital Budget Application, Volume I, physical PDF p. 248.

## Unresolved items

- The located filings do not provide an as-built route and site diagram for the 2004 trunked proposal.
- The located filings do not identify every P25 site, talkgroup, backhaul route, radio model, antenna, or fleet-vehicle assignment.
- The public records do not establish the provenance of individual surplus Quantars or other repeaters later used by amateur-radio groups.
