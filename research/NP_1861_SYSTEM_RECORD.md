# 1861 System Record

## Cape Race vessel-reporting notice

The April 9 *St. John's Daily News* prints a company notice that scheduled a vigilant watch at Cape Race from April 15. Vessels seeking a telegraph report to their owners were to show their flag and number. The listed charge was $1.50 for a Newfoundland vessel and the regular telegraphic toll for a foreign vessel. English dispatches were routed through R. Stuart at 10 Exchange Street East, Liverpool.

The notice states a start date. It does not report that the watch began on April 15.

## Chapel Arm and Harbor Main damage

The June 7 *St. John's Daily News* reports that two boat crews landed near Chapel Arm, Trinity Bay, and removed more than half a mile of telegraph wire. The company did not have enough replacement wire. An operator carried business between the two ends of the break, and A. M. Mackay told the New York agency to stop sending European business. The same report states that poles at Harbor Main were cut down and carried off for nearly half a mile.

The June 21 issue identifies the Chapel Arm event as June 3. It reports that two Bay de Verde skiffs entered Chapel Arm and that members of their crews walked along the line. The article states that skippers Henry Blundon and Laurence Hyde did not leave their vessels. It does not establish who removed the wire.

## Repair logistics

The June 21 article states that the Chapel Arm line remained disconnected until the following Friday. It reports that the *Blue Jacket* was sent from St. John's with wire, insulators and other required material. The article does not print the reconnection date.

## Delayed diplomatic traffic

An official message printed on March 4, 1862 states that wire cutting delayed important communications from the British Minister at Washington to a Secretary of State in the latter part of November 1861. In early December, the telegraph company asked the Governor to explain the delay to the British government. The exact cutting location and date are not stated.
