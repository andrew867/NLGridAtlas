# 1805 System Record

## St. John's defence arrangement

In a July 18 despatch, Erasmus Gower described the harbour defence system as protecting the entrance and providing a strong post on Signal Hill. He reported that the town was too far from Signal Hill to benefit directly from that post. Fort Townshend and Fort William were the remaining town defences, but he described both as nearly decayed and not tenable. He also reported that an enemy landing at Bay Bulls or Torbay could force the garrison to Signal Hill and leave the town exposed.

Gower valued the St. John's buildings, stores, provisions, fish and oil at more than £500,000. He reported that at least 12,000 people depended on the town's provisions for the coming winter. These are the governor's reported estimates, not a later valuation.

## St. John's volunteer companies

The same despatch reported an association formed before Gower's arrival for defence of the town. It consisted of five companies containing 250 men. Major General Skerrett had furnished them with arms and they were learning the exercise. Gower recommended rations while drilling and assistance with uniform clothing. He did not count the association as an effective force at the time of the report.

## Signal Hill works report

On July 20, Gower forwarded the Commanding Royal Engineer's report on the defences of St. John's. He called attention to the report's twelfth page, where the engineer questioned whether he had authority to complete proposed works on Signal Hill. Gower requested full instructions. The surviving volume records the forwarding letter but does not reproduce the engineer's report, so the proposed works are not reconstructed.

## Placentia and Bonavista jails

On October 20, Gower reported drawing £125 from the Treasury toward building the Placentia jail and £150 for the same service at Bonavista. The despatch records authorized payments toward construction, not completion of either building.

## Convoy departures

Gower reported leaving St. John's on October 26 with a homeward convoy of 33 sail. HMS *Jamaica* left the same day with 36 sail for Portugal. Further convoys were appointed for England and Portugal on November 20 and December 20. The November 28 despatch records the two October departures and later appointments, but not completion of the later sailings.

## Placentia fortification survey

Gower sent Captain Ross, the Commanding Royal Engineer, to survey the Placentia works. He reported that the battery had no parapet, was low enough to be cleared by one round of grape shot from the sea and was commanded by several heights ashore. He considered the fortification ineffective. The annual Ordnance establishment, including a storekeeper, carpenter, mason, smith and barrack master, was reported at no less than £500, excluding stores. That year's stores were computed at more than £1,000.

## Burin jail, court and worship building

Merchants and other inhabitants of Burin contributed toward a jail and sought an assessment for the balance. Gower authorized a small assessment on boats and servants employed in the western Placentia Bay fishery and directed that Burin court fines and forfeitures also support the work. The planned upper floor was to serve as a place of worship and occasionally as a court house. He estimated the total at £700 or £800 and requested authority to draw £125 after completion. The despatch records a financed proposal, not a completed building.
