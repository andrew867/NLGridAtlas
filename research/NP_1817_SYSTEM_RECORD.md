# 1817 System Record

## Harbour signal and ice

The January 4 *Newfoundland Mercantile Journal* reports that a shore signal was made on January 3 for a brig north of St. John's. The vessel drifted south past the harbour in a field of ice with its sails handed. Boats from shore could not reach it. The notice does not identify the signal apparatus or the vessel.

## April fire, alarm and failed signal visibility

The April 8 *Royal Gazette* reports a fire near the road to Best's farm on the night of April 6. Three buildings were destroyed. Joseph Cressy's wife, four children and servant died, except for one child recovered from the ruins. Captain Johnson, his wife and six children also died. The report states that the Armed Association guard at the Court House gave the first general alarm. Heavy drifting snow prevented the garrison from seeing their signals. It says no cause could be assigned.

## Light Establishment operating account

The April 18 *Mercantile Journal* prints the Light Establishment account kept by treasurer N. Gill. The ledger covers 1812 through March 1817. It records payments for oil, wick, a lamp, carpenter work, a buoy, printing, carriage and attendants. The 1817 entries include 50 pounds to Charles Grant for one year's attendance on the lights, one pound for carriage of oil, 4 pounds 3 shillings 4 pence for one month's attendance and 13 pounds 12 shillings to Mary Hume for wick. The account closes at 330 pounds 15 shillings 7 pence, including a balance of 262 pounds 0 shillings 3 pence in the treasurer's hands.

The printed account uses both "Light House" and "Lights." It does not identify the site or assign each expense to a particular light.

## Garrison, fort and ship salutes

The June 6 *Mercantile Journal* reports the June 4 observance of the King's birthday. Ships in port displayed colours at 8:00 a.m. At noon the garrison paraded, the artillery fired a royal salute, all guns at the different forts discharged and a feu de joie closed the sequence. HMS *Pike* fired a separate royal salute at 1:00 p.m. after being decorated with colours.

## November 7 fire

Governor Francis Pickmore's November 12 dispatch states that the fire began at 10:00 p.m. on November 7 in a small house beside Water Street near the 1816 fire site. It spread to provision stores below Water Street and destroyed the Court House above it. The Navy and Army stopped the western spread after a large store beside the Governor's Wharf and houses on the east side of Church Hill were taken down. The fire continued east through the storehouses from the Governor's Wharf to the Ordnance Wharf. Two storehouses beside the Ordnance and Commissariat departments were demolished before the fire stopped in that direction.

Pickmore reports that Holloway Street, Gamber Street, much of Duckworth Street and both sides of Water Street between those limits were destroyed. He counts upwards of 130 dwelling houses, exclusive of wharves and storehouses, and estimates property losses at 400,000 to 500,000 pounds. He did not know of any deaths. The November 11 *Royal Gazette* separately reports nearly a quarter mile burned, an area 300 yards wide, 13 mercantile establishments, at least 135 dwellings and not fewer than 1,100 occupants. These are two dated reports, not one reconciled survey.

The Governor temporarily prohibited provision exports from St. John's. The *Royal Gazette* lost most of its printing materials, but the proprietor of the *Mercantile Journal* offered the use of his office and type so publication could continue.

## November 21 fire and cause conflict

The November 25 *Royal Gazette* reports that the November 21 fire began shortly after 3:00 a.m. at Huie, Reid and Company. It states that 55 dwellings were destroyed, more than 500 people required shelter and property losses were at least 150,000 pounds. The same issue prints a Grand Jury resolution that, after considering the evidence, attributed this fire solely to accident.

The same physical page reports a separate attempted ignition on November 25 in a lower room between Duckworth and Water streets. Shavings, a bedstead and the ceiling were burning, and burning brands were found among the shavings. A woman seen leaving through a window and a man believed to be her companion were in custody. The report says the circumstances raised strong suspicion of design, but the investigation was not complete.

Governor Pickmore's January 1, 1818 proclamation later described the November 7 and November 21 fires as unexplained and connected them with another attempted fire. The Grand Jury's November 25 opinion and the Governor's later description are retained with their dates and issuers. Neither is used to prove that a person deliberately started either November fire.

## Post Office continuity

Simon Solomon's notice dated December 26 and printed in the December 30 *Royal Gazette* states that the Post Office moved temporarily to the shop lately occupied by Thomas Phippard, watchmaker. Solomon says he was burnt out on November 7 and lost the fruits of more than 25 years' industry. He also continued clock and watch work at the temporary shop.

## Fortifications and harbour works

The Commanding Engineer's December 13 report, transmitted by Pickmore on December 20, records the following conditions:

- Fort Townshend was dilapidated. No reinstatement was contemplated. Its harbour-facing sea battery was in repair. A working artillery laboratory near the powder magazine also stored travelling magazines when they were not in service.
- Fort William had no material alteration. Its Gate Battery, which cooperated in defence of the harbour and Narrows, was in repair, although some ordnance had been removed.
- The Ordnance Yard had received only occasional repairs to offices and buildings.
- The wharfinger's house at King's Wharf had been dismantled during the November 7 fire to protect the Ordnance store. The report records an application for a new site on the beach opposite the wharf. A low stone and slated building on the former site was the alternative.
- Signal Hill had orders against new work. Repairs were limited to those considered necessary. The stockade was incomplete along a 60-yard section on the edge of a cliff. Repairs had been made to buildings and the Contingent Branch barracks. The other sea batteries were reported in repair and effective.
- Fort Amherst was reported in good repair. A wooden structure inside the magazine reduced damp only partly. The fence along the path between batteries had been removed.
- Frederick's Battery was reported in good repair, although the magazine remained rather damp.

## Court House, Government House and rebuilding proposals

Pickmore's December 22 dispatch states that the courts continued after the November 7 destruction of the Court House. He reports a proposed remote site for a combined Court House, jail and offices. He proposed obtaining a framed building from Nova Scotia or Prince Edward Island and using stone for the lower part. Stone and limestone held by the Ordnance Department at Signal Hill for a stopped Martello tower project were proposed for the work. These were plans and material proposals, not completed buildings.

The same dispatch says no suitable winter residence for the governor could be hired in or near St. John's. Pickmore was making the existing Government House habitable, but reported the rear as too weak for severe weather without a brick or stone front to the northwest. He also proposed rebuilding regulation because he attributed much of the fire spread to narrow streets and contiguous houses. The dispatch does not record that those regulations were enacted in 1817.

## Newspaper and manuscript coverage

The review covered all 25 surviving 1817 files listed in the MUN DAI *Newfoundland Mercantile Journal* collection and all 68 listed *Royal Gazette* issues and supplements. Together they comprise 356 physical PDF pages. The Mercantile files run from January 1 to June 13. The Gazette files run from January 14 to December 30. The archive filenames mark the October 14 supplement as missing a page and the December 30 supplement as possibly missing a page.

The official manuscript review also covered the 115 physical pages of *Despatches from the Governor of Newfoundland to the Secretary of State for the Colonies, vol. 3, 1816-1817*. The volume includes the November fire dispatches, the December fortification report and the December rebuilding proposals.
