# 1864 System Record

## Operating telegraph network

*Hutchinson's Newfoundland Directory for 1864-65* lists the New York, Newfoundland, and London Telegraph Company's operating stations and tariffs.

The Newfoundland stations were St. Johns, Harbor Grace, Cape Race, Verulam, Brigus, Carbonear, Bay Roberts, Lamanche, Long Harbor, Conn River, Bay du Nord, White Bear Bay, Grandy's Brook, Garia and Port au Basque. The Cape Breton stations were Aspe Bay, Ingonish, Baddock and Port Hood. These spellings follow the directory.

The tariff between St. Johns, Brigus, Bay Roberts, Harbor Grace, Carbonear and Verulam was 25 cents for the first ten words and 2 cents for each additional word. The St. Johns to Port au Basque tariff was $2 for the first ten words and 10 cents for each additional word. The Port Hood to Newfoundland tariff was $3 for the first ten words and 12 cents for each additional word.

The head office was in the Market House Building, St. Johns. Robert W. Lowber was secretary, Alexander M. Mackay was general superintendent and local manager, John Waddell was bookkeeper and Thomas D. Scanlan was chief operator.

## Damage near Holyrood

The November 12 *Day-Book* reprinted an editorial concerning the recent destruction of telegraph wires near Holyrood. The report gives no event date, damaged length, restoration date or responsible party.

## Caplin Bay pole removal

The November 23 *Day-Book* prints a Ferryland report dated November 21. It states that one telegraph pole on the Caplin Bay road was cut down with an axe and carried away. On the same page, the Chief Justice refers to two recent cases involving the cutting of telegraph wires. The report does not identify the second incident or give a restoration date.
