# 1818 System Record

## Arson response after the 1817 St. John's fires

Governor Francis Pickmore's January 1 proclamation states that somebody had attempted to set fire to William Hogan's house in Maggotty Cove. It connects the attempt with the unexplained fires of November 7 and November 21, 1817. The proclamation offers 300 pounds for evidence that leads to conviction of anybody who set fire to a house. Merchants and principal inhabitants offer a further 1,000 pounds. A separate 100-pound reward covers evidence of an unlawful association or conspiracy to burn all or part of the town.

## Electrical resuscitation guidance

The March 10 *Royal Gazette* reprints resuscitation guidance under the heading "Suspended Animation." For apparent death caused by lightning, the article calls electricity the most effective remedy for restoring the energy of the brain and recommends obtaining an electrifying machine as soon as possible. The article does not report that a machine was present or used in Newfoundland.

## Portugal Cove fire response

The April 3 *Newfoundland Mercantile Journal* reports a fire at Michael Handlan's dwelling house at Western Point, Portugal Cove. The family escaped through a window with one bed. The house, household property, fishing materials and a store belonging to the same person were destroyed. An adjoining store was pulled down to prevent the fire from reaching nearby buildings. The report attributes the fire to a servant leaving hot ashes in a tub in the porch.

## Harbour salutes and flag display

The July 24 issue reports the arrival of HMS *Ister* with Governor Charles Hamilton. When the ship anchored in the Narrows, the commodore's pendant was struck aboard HMS *Sir Francis Drake*. Ships in port and the garrison fired salutes, and *Ister* returned the salutes after passing the Narrows. After the governor landed at Government Wharf, the flagship and garrison fired the usual salutes. At sunset, the governor's flag was transferred from *Ister* to *Sir Francis Drake*.

## Light-House Establishment notice

The August 7 issue advertises an anniversary dinner for the "Friends and Supporters of the Light-House Establishment" at the Crown and Anchor Tavern on August 12. The notice does not identify a lighthouse, site, light, keeper, governing body or construction project. It documents the name used by the notice, not an operating lighthouse.

## Fort and Signal Hill fuel tender

A Commissariat notice dated December 9 requests 400 cords of black, yellow and white birch for delivery between May 1 and June 24, 1819. The allocation was 150 cords for Fort William, 200 cords for Fort Townshend and 50 cords for Signal Hill. Bids could cover all or part of the work, but no bid could cover less than 20 cords. Each tender had to name two sureties. The notice appears in the December 24 issue and continued into January 1819.

## Schooner Helen loss

The December 31 issue reports that the schooner *Helen* missed stays just inside North Head while entering the Narrows from Bay Bulls. The vessel anchored with its best bower. A pilot boat then helped put out two more anchors. The agent sent a boat and six men with a warp and kedge anchor, but the wind prevented their use and the men boarded *Helen* to assist there. The stream hawser, best-bower cable and small-bower cable later parted. The vessel struck between the north point and Wash-ball Rocks and sank. The report states that six people died.

The article describes the incident as occurring on the preceding Saturday evening. This record uses the December 31 publication date. Sergeant Philip Herring's January 1819 statement separately identifies the incident date and Fort Amherst signal rule.

## Newspaper coverage

The review covered all 47 surviving 1818 files listed in the MUN DAI *Newfoundland Mercantile Journal* collection and all 13 surviving 1818 files in the *Royal Gazette* collection. Together they comprise 234 physical PDF pages. The *Mercantile Journal* files run from January 9 to December 31. The online collection has no issue dated March 20, March 27, June 12, September 3 or September 17. The *Royal Gazette* files run weekly from January 13 through April 7. Its online run then resumes in January 1828.

The January 9 *Mercantile Journal* prints Simon Solomon's Post Office notice dated December 26, 1817. It belongs to the 1817 record. A December 24 copied report mentions a telegraphic dispatch from Paris, but gives no Newfoundland telegraph company, apparatus, route, station or operation.
