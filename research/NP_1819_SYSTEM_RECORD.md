# 1819 System Record

## Fort and Signal Hill fuel logistics

The January 7, 1819 *Newfoundland Mercantile Journal* repeats a Commissariat tender dated December 9, 1818 for 400 cords of hardwood for the fuel yards at Fort William, Fort Townshend and Signal Hill. The allocation was 150 cords to Fort William, 200 cords to Fort Townshend and 50 cords to Signal Hill. Delivery was required between May 1 and June 24, 1819. Bids could cover all or part of the work, but no bid could be for less than 20 cords. Tenders were due at noon on January 11 and had to name two sureties. The timeline assigns the tender to its December 1818 notice date.

## Pilot regulation and Narrows safety

A January 11 letter from Lloyd's agent Newman W. Hoyles, published on January 14, describes a merchant effort to regulate pilots. The proposed system required pilots to provide security for good conduct and pass an examination before competent persons. Hoyles states that a merchant committee established the arrangement, but some masters declined to use pilots because the rules were not legally enforceable. He argued that 100 pounds spent in the Narrows ten years earlier could have protected lives and property. That statement is an opinion by Hoyles, not a documented expenditure or completed work.

The letter also discusses the loss of the schooner *Helen* in the Narrows on December 26, 1818. Hoyles initially criticized the absence of a Fort Amherst distress signal. On January 26, after further investigation, he withdrew that criticism and stated that no blame appeared to attach to the officer.

The January 28 issue prints Sergeant Philip Herring's account of the incident and his standing orders at Fort Amherst. Herring states that a pilot boat assisted the schooner at about 5 p.m. and used a warp to haul her toward South Head. His orders were to make the distress signal when a vessel was in danger, using a red pendant over a yellow pendant with a gun, and to repeat the guns if the danger was great. He did not make the signal because the schooner had hauled away from the danger. This account documents the signal rule and pilot-boat action. It does not establish the complete signal code for the harbour.

## Electrical explanation printed in St. John's

The January 21 issue prints a general scientific explanation of clouds, thunder and lightning. It states that clouds charged with electric matter could unite and produce an explosion similar to gunpowder, called thunder and lightning. This is evidence of electrical terminology printed for Newfoundland readers. It is not a Newfoundland electrical installation or a local measurement.

## Distress signals and rescues

The March 4 issue reports that HMS *Carnation* drifted close to the St. John's wharves during a heavy southeast gale. The ship fired a gun and made distress signals, then was secured through what the report calls great exertions. The report does not identify the responders or the exact securing method.

The April 8 issue reports that a boat from Freshwater Bay upset while entering the Narrows in a strong south wind. A signal was made at Fort Amherst. Joseph Gribble immediately sent a boat, which saved the two men clinging to the overturned boat and towed their boat into safety.

## Fire-engine arrival

The May 27 Custom-House entry lists the brig *Collins* arriving from Liverpool on May 25 with cargo that included one fire engine. The entry does not name the purchaser, owner, intended operator or installation site. It therefore cannot be assigned to the Ordnance Department engine or Mr. Job's patent engine documented two months later.

## Water Street fire response

The July 29 issue reproduces a report dated July 22 about the Water Street fire of July 19. The fire was discovered between 1 a.m. and 2 a.m. in an empty house recently occupied by George Garland. Dry weather and combustible building materials helped the fire spread. The report states that the buildings, wharves and stores from Codner and Treacy's premises to Mrs. Elliott's were destroyed in about two and a half hours.

The response used at least two named engines. An engine under Mr. Bennett was positioned at the west end of the fire and repeatedly protected Codner and Treacy's dwelling house. Colonel Manners and the troops operated an Ordnance Department engine between Mrs. Elliott's premises and newer buildings to the east. Mr. Job used a small patent engine belonging to his house to wet the shingled roofs on the north side of Water Street. The report credits a 60-foot open space left after the November 1817 fire with helping stop the eastward spread. It reports that more than 1,100 people were driven from their homes.

The source does not identify the manufacturer, pump type, capacity or water supply of any engine. It also does not connect the fire engine listed in the May customs entry to either engine used in July.

## Newspaper coverage

The review covered all 46 surviving 1819 files listed in the MUN DAI *Newfoundland Mercantile Journal* collection, comprising 182 physical PDF pages. The files run from January 7 to December 30. The online collection has no issue for April 29, July 15, July 22, August 5, October 28 or December 9.

The MUN DAI *Royal Gazette* collection has no digitized 1819 issue. Its online run ends in April 1818 and resumes in January 1828. The MUN DAI *Public Ledger* collection begins in January 1827. The main newspaper list and limited-run newspaper list identify no other digitized collection covering 1819. These collection gaps do not establish that no other 1819 newspaper existed or survives elsewhere.
