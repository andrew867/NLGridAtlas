# 1809 System Record

## King's Wharf fish market

Robert Brine Sr. and Robert Parsons & Co. announced an operating fish market near the King's Wharf. They had formed a company, obtained a room formerly occupied by Norcomb & Henley and fitted out three boats to supply fresh fish daily during the season. The notice describes an operating service, not a proposed market building.

## Ordnance materials

The Office of Ordnance sought five classes of building material for His Majesty's works: 15,000 feet of pine boards with some plank, 12,000 feet of two-inch spruce plank, 4,000 feet of one-and-one-half-inch spruce plank, 60,000 feet of spruce boards and 100,000 prime shingles. The material was to be delivered on the Ordnance Wharf at St. John's. The notice establishes procurement quantities and a delivery point, not completed work.

A separate notice sought 3,000 tons of sound freestone for His Majesty's works. The stone was to work readily with a mallet and chisel, be roughly hewn into right-angled pieces three to five feet long and one foot five inches to one foot ten inches square. Stone resembling Portland Island stone was preferred. Bidders could quote delivery to the Ordnance Wharf or aboard vessels at the quarry. This was a tender; the notice does not establish delivery.

## Crow's Nest Powder Magazine

A July 13 notice invited proposals to build a Powder Magazine at Crow's Nest. A plan and description were available at N. W. Hoyles's, and proposals were to be received at Merchants' Hall. The notice fixes the intended location and confirms that a design existed, but it does not establish an award or completed building.

## Harbour signals and passes

A May 18 General Order states that vessels had attempted to leave St. John's without the customary signals or their passes from Fort William. Some vessels leaving at night had ignored repeated hails from the batteries, causing trouble for the garrison and a waste of government powder. The order required merchant vessels to add a night signal to the existing day signals, obtain passes from the Collector of Customs and comply with the usual regulations. Guards at Fort William and Amherst Tower were ordered not to allow contrary departures.

## Post Office letter carriage

Governor Holloway's October 24 proclamation authorized Mr. Solomon to pay one penny per letter to the master or other person of a vessel that brought letters to Newfoundland and delivered them to the St. John's Post Office. Solomon could charge two pence for each letter delivered from the office. The proclamation states that Solomon's appointment as Post-Master had previously been sanctioned by the Postmaster General.

## Public-purpose lottery

The Grand Jury recommended a lottery to raise a fund for public purposes. The printed scheme contained 600 tickets at five dollars each, 59 prizes with a stated total of 1,800 dollars and 541 blanks. A November 2 notice said the tickets were nearly disposed of and scheduled the drawing to begin at Merchants' Hall on December 1. The notice establishes the scheme and scheduled drawing, not the amount ultimately raised or the works funded.

## Harbour Grace Court House, Bay Bulls jail and Ships' Rooms register

Governor Holloway's November 25 report states that the Harbour Grace Court House was quite completed and that he had drawn the £500 allowed for it. The same physical page records £100 paid to William Carter for making a register of Ancient Fishing Ships' Rooms from the lease funds, and £80 paid toward purchasing a house adapted as a jail at Bay Bulls.

The report states that Holloway perambulated St. John's on October 4 with the Chief Justice, High Sheriff, magistrates and peace officers to preserve the remembered boundaries of the Ancient Fishing Ships' Rooms shown on the town plan. He found many encroachments but did not consider himself authorized to remove buildings apparently sanctioned by his predecessors.

## Land survey, Signal Hill and Placentia fort

Holloway reported that inhabitants were enlarging land enclosures. He proposed an annual salary of £50 for Lionel Chancey as King's Surveyor so the enclosed acreage could be established, and proposed stopping further enclosures of land that was not leased, granted or used for the fishery. These were proposals, not a completed survey.

The same report states that Captain Durnford of the Engineers prepared a return of the St. John's fortifications and works in progress. Signal Hill works had made little progress because the regiment could not supply enough labour to assist the artificers. Placentia fort remained in the previous year's condition because no dismantling instructions had arrived. A separate November 26 return describes the Placentia fortification as ruinous and awaiting orders to be dismantled.
