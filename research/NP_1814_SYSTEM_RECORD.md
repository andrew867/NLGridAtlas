# 1814 System Record

## Roads, bridges and fire engines

A St. John's notice dated January 13 proposed a lottery to raise money for repairs to the town's roads, bridges and fire engines. It offered 1,000 tickets at 20 shillings each. The printed schedule allocated 600 pounds to prizes and left 400 pounds for public purposes.

## Post Office collection system

A February 10 Post Office notice states that the number of letters brought to the office was small compared with the number received at the port. It ended delivery to individual addresses, replacing it with a list posted outside the office. Recipients were to collect their letters and pay on delivery.

Governor Richard Goodwin Keats reported a separate packet-mail failure on September 30. He said many Newfoundland letters sent from England through Halifax had been left in the Halifax office even though he had sent a King's ship expressly for the mail. He proposed that Newfoundland letters be made up as a separate sealed bag in London so they would not have to be sorted at Halifax.

A St. John's Post Office notice dated December 1 sought a steady, sober man with a letter-carrier to deliver letters to local recipients. The notice was printed on January 5, 1815.

## Ordnance materials

An Ordnance notice dated March 10 sought 400 hogsheads of English or Sydney coal, 300 rails 18 feet long and 3 inches in diameter at the small end, 3,000 pickets 7 feet long and 2.5 inches in diameter at the small end, 100 cords of large straight firewood, 4,000 superficial feet of 3-inch plank, and one 50-foot flagstaff spar 10 inches in diameter at the small end. The materials were to go to the Ordnance Yard by May 15, except for the coal, which was to go to King's Wharf by July 1.

The same page carries an Ordnance Barrack Office notice for 3,000 hogsheads of English or Sydney coal and 200 cords of firewood. Both notices record solicitations, not completed deliveries.

## Ordnance wharf operations

Rules dated March 19 required the Ordnance wharf and platform to be kept clean, free of stored lumber and open to the landing place. The large gate was to open only for service, while the wicket gate was to remain open from sunrise until 9:00 p.m., or later when required. Department vessels could remain at the wharf only while loading or discharging, and the department's boats were to be moored every evening before dark.

The wharf keeper was also directed to prevent floating ice from damaging the wharf and to stop coal, sand and stones from falling into the water during loading or shipping because the deposits reduced the harbour depth.

## Garrison fuel and truckage

A Commissariat notice dated May 11 sought 2,000 hogsheads of English or Sydney coal for the Commissariat Wharf and 200 cords of firewood divided equally among the fuel yards at Signal Hill, Fort Townshend and Fort William.

A September 15 notice sought 1,000 to 1,500 hogsheads of coal and 200 cords of firewood. It allowed firewood delivery to Signal Hill, Fort Townshend, Fort William or the Commissariat Wharf by February 10, 1815.

The October 27 truckage tender identifies the land distribution routes. It requested separate rates to move coal from King's Wharf to Signal Hill, Fort William and Fort Townshend; firewood to each fort; firewood between Fort William and Signal Hill; and provisions from King's Wharf to each fort and between Fort William and Signal Hill. The contract term was 12 months from November 15.

## Fire response

The June 16 *Royal Gazette* reports that a fire began at about 8:00 a.m. in Doctor Donogan's rooms near the chapel. The building and its contents were destroyed, but the report credits firemen and engines with preventing further spread.

The December 1 issue reports a second fire, on what it calls Monday morning last, in James Duggan's house in front of the Meeting House. Duggan's house and two adjoining tenements burned. The fire reached the west end of the Meeting House, but the report states that the building and its neighbours escaped material damage through exertion and nearly calm weather.

## Chapel sites and government land

Keats's March 19 letter records an application by the Methodist society for land on which to build a chapel. He rejected the three marked sites. Site B formed the principal part of Gallows Hill Ship Room and had been reserved for a proposed public market. Site C was inside a line around Fort Townshend where avoidable building was not proposed, and it lay beside the route used by the garrison to reach the established church. Site A was near the water and a costly wharf being built for the Ordnance and Commissariat departments.

## Record Office, survey and defence works

An April 1 letter states that the Treasury had authorized a Record Office at St. John's. Keats requested two complete cast-iron doors, each 6 feet high by 2 feet 9 inches wide.

Keats's December 29 annual report states that the Engineer Department was collecting and forming materials for contemplated Signal Hill defences. He also reported that two 18-pounder guns had been sent to Ferryland and that small Newfoundland Regiment detachments had been assigned to Ferryland, Burin and St. Lawrence.

The same report records the appointment of Thomas George William Hasstaff to prepare a general survey and plan of Crown grants near St. John's. Hasstaff was engaged for 100 pounds, with two artillery assistants at 1 shilling per day each. The survey was not reported complete until 1815.

The Record Office was deferred because the iron doors did not arrive before frost and construction could not begin without them. Keats also deferred a new gaol and sheriff's house, citing war conditions and the possibility that lumber and material prices would fall.

## Volunteer Rangers

Keats's July 8 report states that the St. John's Volunteer Rangers had held no drills, parades or field day during his absence, that more than two-thirds of the corps had left St. John's, and that the officers could not assemble one company for the sovereign's birthday. He directed that the accounts be closed, the corps' arms and military stores be returned to King's Stores, and further payments be suspended pending instructions. He expressly separated the suspension from any accusation of disloyalty.

## Market building

A notice dated December 29 proposed an oblong market building on the site of the late fire. It specified a central pump, equal stalls with lofts and covered frontage, and space for fresh meat, poultry, vegetables and fish. Construction was to begin after enough stalls had been engaged. The notice was printed on January 5, 1815 and does not establish that the building was completed.
