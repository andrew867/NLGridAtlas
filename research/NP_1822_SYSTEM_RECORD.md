# 1822 System Record

## Speedwell packet communication

The October 10, 1822 *Newfoundland Mercantile Journal* advertises the new packet boat *Speedwell* between Portugal Cove and Harbour Grace on physical PDF p. 3. The boat was to sail twice a week or whenever required. Its new craft was arranged for six oars, and six men could be provided when required.

Letters and packages could be left with Patrick Kough near the Three Crowns on Duckworth Street in St. John's, or with proprietor Michael Dooley at Portugal Cove. The notice undertook their safe carriage and delivery.

## Fire-engine, lighthouse and pilotage governance

The October 24 issue says the colony lacked a lawful means to appoint pilots and prevent unqualified people from acting as pilots. The editorial identifies the fire-engine institution and lighthouse institution among public institutions that had dwindled or were dwindling for want of local authority to make laws and enforce penalties. It does not name the lighthouse or locate the fire engine.

A merchants' notice dated October 31 and printed on November 7 established a private pilotage rule. The signatories would not pay pilotage for vessels that belonged to them or were consigned to them unless the pilot held a certificate from their appointed committee. The pilot also had to provide two respectable sureties for good behaviour, separate from proof of qualification.

## Lively packet communication

The November 14 issue prints a notice dated November 13, 1822 for the new packet boat *Lively* on physical PDF p. 3. The boat ran between Portugal Cove and Harbour Grace twice a week. It was arranged for six oars, with six men available when required.

The notice set passenger fares at five shillings for ladies and gentlemen and four shillings for tradesmen and labourers. Hire of the boat cost 30 shillings, and letters cost nine pence each. Letters and packages could be left at Mr. Lee's printing office in St. John's or with proprietor James Neary at Portugal Cove.

## Brig and harbour signal report

The December 19 issue reports that a brig appeared south of St. John's harbour on Monday afternoon, December 16. A hard, squally north-west wind was blowing. About two hours later, the signal was hauled down after the brig passed northward. The report supposed that the vessel was bound for Conception Bay.

The report does not identify the signal's mechanism, operator or location.

## Dated boundaries

The *Lively* notices printed early in 1822 are dated November 29, 1821. They belong to the 1821 record.

The reviewed files did not provide a supported 1822 Newfoundland lighthouse project, electric telegraph, installed electrical circuit, operating electrical apparatus or local lightning-damage event.

## Newspaper search

The review covered all 42 surviving 1822 files listed in the MUN DAI *Newfoundland Mercantile Journal* collection, comprising 168 physical PDF pages. The files run from January 3 to December 26. The online collection has no issue for January 31, August 8, August 15, August 29, September 5, September 12, September 19, September 26, October 31 or December 12.

The MUN DAI *Royal Gazette* collection has no digitized 1822 issue. Its online run ends in April 1818 and resumes in January 1828. The MUN DAI main newspaper list and limited-run newspaper list identify no other digitized collection covering 1822. These collection gaps do not establish that no other 1822 newspaper existed or survives elsewhere.
