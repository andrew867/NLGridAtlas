# 1855 System Record

## Bay Despair construction

By June 13, the *Victoria* had carried labourers and supplies to Bay Despair and other points on the inland telegraph route. About two hundred men were employed. The company planned to hire more after minor repairs to the vessel.

## Cable expedition arrival

The *James Adger* reached St. John's on August 14 after calling at Port aux Basques. The *Sarah L. Bryant*, chartered at Liverpool to carry the Cape Ray to Cape North cable, had not arrived when the *James Adger* called at Port aux Basques.

## Cape Ray landing and failed Gulf crossing

The expedition changed its Newfoundland landing from Port aux Basques to Cape Ray Cove. A cable house was erected, and the cable end was landed on August 23.

An August 25 attempt was abandoned after a collision strained the cable. The cable was cut when the bark lost its anchor and drifted toward a reef. The splice made the next day was rejected, two miles of cable were abandoned, and a new end was hauled ashore.

A second attempt began August 29. A kink broke the cable after roughly twenty miles and caused an eight-hour delay. Paying out resumed, but a southeast gale endangered the bark. The crew cut the cable after forty miles had been paid out. Thirty-four miles remained aboard, and the vessel was about thirty miles from Cape North.

## Cape North survey

By September 13, the *Victoria* had visited Cape North. Company officers surveyed the site and made arrangements for a later cable-laying attempt. The vessel returned about 120 workers after calling at several inland-line locations.

## Inland route inspection

On September 21, the *Public Ledger* reported that F. N. Gisborne had left St. John's with J. T. Neville to inspect the route to Cape Ray. The article calls this Gisborne's second journey and refers to his 1851 survey through Piper's Hole and Cape Ray.

## Inland construction contracts

Chief engineer Charles B. Ellis issued a November 17 notice seeking contractors for bridle roads, bridges, poles, braces and wire from Piper's Hole to Cape Ray and from St. John's to Trepassey. The notice proposed January 1, 1856 for the roads and bridges and March 10 for cut and stripped poles and braces. It advertised six weeks of work for four hundred men.
