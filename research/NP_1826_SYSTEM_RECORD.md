# 1826 System Record

## Conception Bay packet communication

The February 2 *Newfoundland Mercantile Journal* prints a notice dated January 26 on physical PDF p. 3. The owners laid the *Express* up until spring and purchased a more suitable boat for winter crossings between Harbour Grace and Portugal Cove. If navigation closed, a letter carrier was to travel around Conception Bay. James Clift at St. John's and William Innott at Harbour Grace were the agents.

The May 11 issue prints an April 4 notice on physical PDF p. 3. The fitted *Express* packet operated between Harbour Grace and Portugal Cove under an experienced master. It left Harbour Grace at 09:00 on Monday, Wednesday and Saturday, then returned from Portugal Cove at 10:00 the next morning except Sunday and during bad weather. Housekeepers and planters paid 10 shillings, servants and children paid 5 shillings, a letter cost 1 shilling, and parcels were charged in proportion. James Clift and William Innott remained the agents.

The September 7 issue prints a notice of the same date on physical PDF p. 3. The packet reduced its single-letter charge from 1 shilling to 6 pence for the rest of the season. Double letters were charged in proportion.

The December 14 issue prints an October 12 notice on physical PDF p. 1. For the rest of the season, the letter carrier left St. John's at 07:00 every Tuesday, Thursday and Saturday. The boat left Harbour Grace one hour earlier on the previous morning. The notice states that the earlier times were intended to complete the passage by daylight.

The January 5 issue reprints an earlier *Express* operating notice on physical PDF p. 1. It is retained as antecedent evidence and does not establish a new 1826 service start.

## Western Ward fire response and night watch

The November 9 issue publishes Western Fire and Safety Company rules on physical PDF p. 3. The stated purposes were fire suppression and a nightly watch in St. John's Western Ward. The organization comprised 100 members, including 3 wardens, 2 captains, 2 engineers, 25 hatchet-men and 68 assistants.

The rules identify an engine, engine-house, keys, lanterns, speaking trumpets, ladders, cowls, buckets, saws, hatchets, a chain and a hook ladder. Wardens and captains directed the response. Captains and engineers were to proceed on every fire alarm. Night watchmen carried a lantern and rattle. The rattle was reserved for fire or robbery alarms. Separate winter and summer watch hours were printed.

The surviving fragment does not establish the company's adoption or founding date. The timeline therefore uses the November 9 publication date.

## Claim boundaries

No supported named 1826 Newfoundland lighthouse project, electric telegraph, installed electrical circuit, operating electrical apparatus or local lightning-damage event was found in the reviewed files. Foreign electrical and telegraph reports, property advertisements using Signal Hill as a location, and advertisements for loose lamps were excluded.

## Newspaper search

The review covered all 36 surviving 1826 files listed in the MUN DAI *Newfoundland Mercantile Journal* collection, comprising 144 physical PDF pages. The current MUN DAI main newspaper list starts the *Public Ledger* collection in 1827. The *Newfoundland Mercantile Journal* is the only collection on that main list that covers 1826. This collection boundary does not establish that no other 1826 newspaper existed or survives elsewhere.

Repeated advertisements were treated as continuity evidence, not separate operating events.
