# 1833 System Record

## Fort Amherst fog warning

The Governor's January 10 message to the House of Assembly states that a gun was fired occasionally from Fort Amherst when fog lay on the coast. Its purpose was to warn vessels approaching St. John's harbour. The message explains that Fort Amherst lay too far inside the harbour for the sound to carry far when onshore wind accompanied fog.

This is an official contemporary description reproduced by the *Public Ledger*. It is not an operating log and gives no firing schedule, gun type or range.

## Cape Spear proposal

The same message states that Cape Spear became a source of concern to approaching vessels when the breeze was strong. It proposed an ordinary light and a heavy gun at Cape Spear. The Governor said that they could be established and maintained at moderate expense.

The proposal predates the June 1834 act that organized the Fort Amherst and Cape Spear lighthouse system. It does not establish construction or operation in 1833.

## Lighthouse sites and Narrows moorings

The St. John's Chamber of Commerce annual report, dated August 7, says that the Governor directed the Chamber's attention to coastal lighthouse construction near the end of the previous year. A committee identified what it considered the best stations and reported them to the Governor. The published report does not name those stations.

The Chamber also reported that most of its available funds had been spent laying mooring buoys in the Narrows. A November gale damaged the mooring lighter and increased the cost. The report credited Captain Bonifant of the Royal Navy with assistance.

## Legislative, telegraph and electrical record

The 78-page 1833 statute volume was searched and its act headings reviewed. No lighthouse authorization was located. The relevant financing and governance act appears in the 1834 volume.

No named 1833 Newfoundland telegraph instrument, company, station or circuit was located in the four local newspaper collections searched. Newspaper uses of *telegraph* describe foreign optical systems or newspaper titles. Electricity and magnetism appear in foreign science reports, not in a documented Newfoundland communication or power installation.
