# 1852 System Record

## Conception Bay service

The St. John's and Carbonear Telegraph Company's first annual report gives the first operating dates from its St. John's office. The first message to Brigus was sent on March 8. The first message to Harbor Grace was sent on March 10.

The report states that wires, posts and insulators were damaged at several points soon afterward. It records repeated damage during the year and repair costs of £53 10s 4d, excluding travel. It does not identify each incident date or location.

## Cape Ray proposal

The March 20 *Times* reported F. N. Gisborne's schedule and labour proposal. The Cape Ray line and mainland connection were expected to take about fifteen months. The article discusses a possible labour force of 1,000 men for the overland work. It does not establish that those workers were hired or that the schedule was achieved.

## Company statute

Chapter 6 incorporated the Newfoundland Electric Telegraph Company on June 14. Authorized capital was £100,000 in 1,000 shares of £100.

The act authorized a trunk line from St. John's toward Cape Ray, a line toward Trepassey or another point on the southwestern coast, and branch lines to towns and villages. These routes could not interfere with the St. John's and Carbonear company's lines.

The act connected the telegraph and road works. The public bridle road from Ship Harbour Road to Cape Ray or Port aux Basques had to be at least four feet wide. Bridges had to be at least eight feet wide. Marsh drainage had to be at least twelve feet wide. After inspection, the company could receive £1,500 for each 100 miles.

Chapter 7 protected the physical telegraph plant. It names station houses, watch houses, posts, rails, wire, machines, machinery, apparatus and connected works. Wilful and malicious damage or interruption was a felony punishable by no more than one year of imprisonment with hard labour.

## Regional cable work

The July 16 *Public Ledger* reported that Gisborne had contracted with R. S. Newall for a Cape Tormentine cable. It also reported a contract for a Cape Ray to East Point cable in the following spring. These were contract and schedule reports, not completion evidence.

On November 12, the *Ellen Gisborne* left Charlottetown towing the *Eliza*, which carried the Cape Tormentine to Carlton Head cable. The steamer's screw propeller contacted the cable vessel's chain. The steamer continued under sail. The cable vessel returned to Charlottetown.

The December 8 *Times* reports that the cable was laid on the night of Monday, November 22. Rough and heavy weather prolonged the work to eleven hours. A battery left at the far end allowed an immediate test, and the article reports that the cable worked.

The same December 8 paragraph prints November 4 as the *Ellen Gisborne*'s return date. That date precedes the stated November 22 completion. The return date remains unresolved.

## Damage response

A company notice dated October 16 offered £20 for information leading to the apprehension of anyone who had injured telegraph wires, posts or insulators. It does not identify an incident date or location.
