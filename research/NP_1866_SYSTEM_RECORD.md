# 1866 System Record

## 1866 Atlantic cable

The July 31, 1866 *Newfoundland Express* reports that St. John's received telegraphic notice on Friday morning of the *Great Eastern* at Heart's Content and the successful laying of the Atlantic cable. The same page describes public celebrations in St. John's.

The expedition diary on physical PDF page 1 preserves the engineering programme that Samuel Canning proposed and Richard Glass approved. It lists:

- 2,724 cable miles taken out for the 1866 expedition;
- 1,670 miles from Valentia to Heart's Content;
- 1,960 cable miles required for that route;
- 600 miles from the end of the 1865 cable to Heart's Content;
- 697.5 cable miles required to recover, splice and complete the 1865 route;
- 66.5 cable miles left after both programme allowances.

These are programme quantities printed before the expedition was complete. They are not used as surveyed as-laid lengths.

The machinery description on the same page identifies a 40 horsepower engine for the paying-out and reversing drum and another 40 horsepower engine for the grappling machine. The diary also describes continuous insulation testing during paying out.

## Cape Ray to Cape North cable fault

A July 27 report printed in *The Times* states that Cyrus Field went ashore before the *Great Eastern* entered Heart's Content. He intended to telegraph St. John's to hire a vessel to repair the cable reported broken between Cape Ray, Newfoundland, and Cape North, Cape Breton Island.

This is a separate regional cable fault. The report does not state when the cable failed or when it returned to service.

## Recovery of the 1865 Atlantic cable

The September 4 *Newfoundland Express* prints a Heart's Content telegram dated September 2. It reports that the *Great Eastern* hooked the 1865 cable at 8 o'clock that morning and was steaming to Heart's Content.

The September 15 issue separately prints news received through the 1865 Atlantic cable after its recovery on September 2. A September 2 dispatch states that the cable was working and that 17 messages had been received during the hour. Chief Electrician Willoughby Smith certified that the printed telegrams had been sent through the 1865 Atlantic cable since its recovery.

The two reports establish recovery and message traffic. They do not provide a formal commissioning certificate.

## New Gulf cable operation

The September 12 *Courier* reports that HMS *Terrible* and the steamship *Medway* had arrived in St. John's from Heart's Content. It states that they were leaving with HMS *Lily* for the purpose of laying a new Gulf cable.

The report establishes a cable-laying operation in progress. It does not identify the Gulf cable endpoints or report completion. The source does not explicitly state that this work repaired the Cape Ray to Cape North fault reported in July.
