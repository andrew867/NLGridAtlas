# 1820 System Record

## Pilot-boat assistance and overland letters

The January 20, 1820 *Newfoundland Mercantile Journal* reports the schooner *Active*, Captain Jeffreys, arriving from Halifax bound for St. John's on physical PDF p. 3. The vessel put into Bay Bulls after splitting her sails in a gale. She then went ashore and lost her rudder. A pilot boat went from St. John's to assist her.

The same report states that the Halifax letters carried by *Active* were brought overland on the following Sunday. This is a documented transfer between sea transport and an overland route. The source does not name the carrier or the landing point used for the letters.

## Fort and Signal Hill firewood tender

The same page prints a Commissariat tender dated January 17, 1820 for 400 cords of fir firewood. Delivery was required to three fuel yards between May 1 and June 24, 1820:

- Fort William, 150 cords
- Fort Townshend, 200 cords
- Signal Hill, 50 cords

The wood was to be stacked in piles of at least 10 cords. Tenders were due at noon on January 25. Each contractor needed two sureties and a bond of 100 pounds for every 50 cords.

This notice documents fuel logistics at Signal Hill and the two St. John's forts. It does not identify a signalling apparatus at Signal Hill.

## Fort William and Methodist Chapel fires

The February 24, 1820 issue reports two fires on physical PDF p. 3. A house behind Fort William, owned by the Honourable William Carter and occupied by Sylvester Nugent and three poor families, burned to the ground. The report states that prompt assistance could not stop the fire. It attributes the probable cause to a chimney spark that reached hay in the loft.

The Methodist Chapel was discovered on fire later the same night. Richard Perchard and his family lived opposite and helped save the building. The report states that cinders raked from the stove into a pan heated the floor below until it ignited. The alarm was given immediately. The stove had fallen, the floor and beams had burned over an area about five feet square, and the fire had begun to catch the pulpit railings.

## Newspaper search

The review covered all 46 surviving 1820 files listed in the MUN DAI *Newfoundland Mercantile Journal* collection, comprising 184 physical PDF pages. The files run from January 13 to December 28. The online collection has no issue for January 6, February 10, March 2, June 22, August 24 or November 9.

The MUN DAI *Royal Gazette* collection has no digitized 1820 issue. Its online run ends in April 1818 and resumes in January 1828. The MUN DAI *Public Ledger* collection begins in January 1827. The main newspaper list and limited-run newspaper list identify no other digitized collection covering 1820. These collection gaps do not establish that no other 1820 newspaper existed or survives elsewhere.
