# 1849 System Record

## Electric telegraph demonstration

On December 18, the *Public Ledger* reviewed Mr. Humphrey's lectures on electricity and electro-magnetism in St. John's. The article described a working miniature electric telegraph operated by a man who had established a line in a neighbouring province.

The model used a perforated paper strip laid along a table. Telegraph apparatus stood at one end and a model station at the other. When the operator applied power, the article said that characters were dotted onto paper at the receiving point.

The lecture also included a miniature sawmill. An electro-magnetic control regulated its speed. Other demonstrations showed magnetic and galvanic effects.

## Cable proposals

The *Public Ledger* considered a submarine wire between Newfoundland and the nearest point of British North America possible. It also discussed an American proposal for a submarine wire from southwestern Ireland with Newfoundland as the direct point of communication.

The article did not name Cape Ray, Cape Breton or F. N. Gisborne. It reported a working model and cable proposals, not an operating Newfoundland telegraph network.

## Later route attribution

On November 17, 1855, the *Newfoundland Express* returned to the 1849 lecture while disputing who originated the Newfoundland telegraph project. It stated that Humphrey had been connected with a New Brunswick telegraph line and had met Gisborne in Halifax. It attributed to the December 1849 lecture a proposed line from St. John's to Cape Ray, a submarine cable from Cape Ray to Cape Breton and a submarine line from St. John's to southwestern Ireland.

The 1855 account says Gisborne had explained the plan to Humphrey and had asked him to introduce it in Newfoundland. These are retrospective origin claims. The contemporary 1849 article confirms the lecture, working model and general mainland and Atlantic cable proposals, but not the named Cape Ray route or the Gisborne attribution.
