# 1830 System Record

## Conception Bay packet communication

The January 7 *Newfoundlander* prints three operating packet notices on physical PDF p. 1.

The *Express* notice gives a regular schedule between Harbour Grace and Portugal Cove. It left Harbour Grace at 09:00 on Monday, Wednesday and Friday, and Portugal Cove at noon on the next day. The published fares were 10 shillings for a cabin passenger, 5 shillings for a steerage passenger and 6 pence for a single letter. J. Clift at St. John's and T. Ridley at Harbour Grace were the agents.

Matthew Guswell stated that he had launched a packet boat built to carry letters and passengers. It left Portugal Cove for Carbonear at 11:00 on Monday, Cubits at 11:00 on Wednesday and Harbour Grace at 11:00 on Friday. Return departures left each destination at 09:00 on Tuesday, Thursday and Saturday respectively. The published fares were 10 shillings for ladies and gentlemen, 5 shillings for other passengers and 6 pence for a letter. The schedule was subject to wind and weather.

James Doyle stated that the *Dart* would continue between Carbonear and Portugal Cove until the end of the year. It left Carbonear on Monday and Thursday, and its St. John's messenger connection operated on Tuesday evening and Saturday morning. Doyle stated that he would deliver letters and parcels personally. The published passenger fares were 10 shillings for ladies and gentlemen, and 5 shillings for servants and children.

The September 21 *Public Ledger* prints two later operating notices on physical PDF p. 1.

A notice dated April 17 states that the *Express* had undergone a thorough repair and resumed its usual trips between Harbour Grace and Portugal Cove. It left Harbour Grace at 09:00 on Monday, Wednesday and Friday. It left Portugal Cove at noon on the next day, except Sunday and subject to weather. The service carried passengers, parcels and letters. The published fares were 10 shillings for a cabin passenger, 5 shillings for a steerage passenger, 6 pence for a single letter and 1 shilling for a double letter. Parcels were charged by weight. Henry Winton at St. John's and Robert Oke at Harbour Grace were the agents.

A notice dated May 1 states that the *Arrow*, operated by Thomas Butler, provided a weekly route between Port de Grave and Portugal Cove. It called at Cubits at 08:00 on Wednesday for passengers, letters and parcels from Brigus, then called at Port de Grave for traffic from Port de Grave and Bay Roberts. A letter carrier delivered the traffic in St. John's and accepted return traffic at the *Public Ledger* office. The return sailing left Portugal Cove at 11:00 on Thursday, subject to weather. The published fares were 10 shillings for ladies and gentlemen, 5 shillings for servants and children, and 6 pence for a single letter. Double letters and parcels were charged proportionally.

The notices document scheduled communication routes. The wording *usual trips* does not establish the *Express* service's first year.

## Newspaper search

The *Newfoundlander* collection contains 51 surviving regular 1830 issues and four supplements. The July 15 issue is missing. All 55 surviving items were downloaded for this review.

No named 1830 Newfoundland lighthouse project, telegraph instrument, electrical installation or operating circuit was located in this set. Foreign news and advertisements were not treated as Newfoundland equipment records.
