# 1862 System Record

## Operating company and service

The 1862 *Newfoundland Almanac* lists the New York, Newfoundland, and London Telegraph Company. Peter Cooper was president, Cyrus W. Field was vice-president, Moses Taylor was treasurer and David D. Field was counsel. Alexander M. Mackey was local manager at St. John's and superintendent.

The company notice dated February 19 lists offices at the Market House Buildings in St. John's and the Temperance Hall Building in Harbor Grace. It states that the company had telegraphic connections with cities and towns on the North American continent. A ten-word message from St. John's to Brigus, Bay Roberts, Harbor Grace, Carbonear or Ferryland cost 25 cents. A ship report from Cape Race to St. John's or Harbor Grace cost $1.50. The notice prints the superintendent's surname as Mackay.

## Spaniard's Bay wire case

The Northern Circuit Court report dated May 5 states that the grand jury returned a true bill against Phelan for breaking down telegraph wires near Spaniard's Bay. A true bill is an indictment. The report does not record a conviction or sentence.

## Aquaforte fault

The May 10 *Day-Book* reports that the line near Aquaforte, between Ferryland and Cape Race, was cut on the Tuesday night before publication. A large portion of wire was carried away. A conveyance left St. John's with replacement wire. The report states that communication with Cape Race and Europe was unavailable for a time, but gives no damaged length or return-to-service time.

## South Shore damage cluster

The May 24 *Day-Book* reports that about 30 yards of wire were cut away at Renews on the Sunday night before publication. It also records cuts at Aquaforte and Cape Broyle. The article places all three incidents within two weeks and states that the Aquaforte and Cape Broyle cuts occurred on the same evening within four hours of each other. It gives no exact incident dates or repair status.
