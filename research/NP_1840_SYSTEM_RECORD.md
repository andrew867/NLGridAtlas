# 1840 System Record

## Proposed scientific institution

On April 14, the *Public Ledger* printed a letter about the proposed Newfoundland Literary and Scientific Institution. The writer referred to its provisional committee and its first general meeting, then requested a different meeting date because the announced date was Good Friday.

The notice does not identify electrical apparatus or telegraph work. No source located in this search proves that this proposed institution was the unnamed St. John's society that owned an electrical machine in 1843.

## St. John's to Halifax steam service

An act passed on April 29 authorized £3,000 to help establish steam communication between St. John's and Halifax. The money was to be drawn from the Treasury in three annual instalments of £1,000. Payment required the communication to be established.

On July 28, the *Public Ledger* reported the arrival of the packet brigantine *Roseway* from Halifax. It carried English mail delivered at Halifax by the steamship *Britannia*. The newspaper called this the first regularly established conveyance of mail from Great Britain to Newfoundland under Cunard supervision. It also said that the Halifax to St. John's leg remained a sailing-packet connection and advocated a steam vessel for that leg.

On September 22, the *Public Ledger* reported that the Steam Navigation Company had abandoned its plan to place a steam vessel on the route during the fall. The article said that £7,000 had been remitted from St. John's. It reported that *City of Glasgow*, or another vessel of similar power and dimensions, was expected in the following spring. That expected service remained prospective on this page.

On October 6, the *Public Ledger* printed an Admiralty contract notice dated September 28. It named the fast-sailing British-built vessels *Sandwich* and *Charles Buchan* for future mail carriage between St. John's and Halifax. Departure from Halifax was tied to the arrival of mail steamers from England. The notice limited each packet's stay at St. John's to forty-eight hours.

The sequence separates five different states: legislation, an operating Cunard-connected sailing mail, a failed fall steam-vessel start, a proposed spring start and the later sailing-packet contract.

## Underwater galvanic demolition

On September 22, the *Royal Gazette* described work at the wreck of *Royal George* at Spithead. The charge contained 25 barrels, or 2,250 pounds, of gunpowder at about 70 feet below the surface. Lieutenant Symonds held the connecting wires from a voltaic battery when the firing order was given.

The operation occurred outside Newfoundland. It records electrical-initiation engineering published for local readers, not local equipment or construction.

No named 1840 Newfoundland electric-telegraph line, company, station, construction project or operating circuit was located.
