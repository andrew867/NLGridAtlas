# 1816 System Record

## February 12 St. John's fire

Thomas Coote's February 18 report states that the fire began at about 8:00 p.m. on February 12 in the upper part of Walsh's house near King's Beach. It spread through Gamber Street, Holloway Street, much of Duckworth Street and the north side of Water Street. The Customs House caught fire but was preserved.

Coote reported about 130 houses destroyed, many others damaged, about 120 families displaced and roughly 1,000 people dependent on relief. He estimated the property loss at more than 100,000 pounds. The destroyed buildings included two printing offices in Duckworth Street, Crawford's new buildings and the newly erected Methodist chapel. These are Coote's contemporary estimates, not a later property survey.

Governor Francis Pickmore's December 11 report states that part of a 10,000-pound relief grant was distributed after individual claims were examined. He also gave 300 pounds to the Society for the Poor and hired two vessels to carry about 400 people to Ireland.

## Harbour Grace church fire

The September 3 *Royal Gazette* reports that the Harbour Grace church burned at about midnight on August 28. It states that there were strong suspicions of deliberate ignition and that the magistrates offered a 100-guinea reward. The report says wet weather and a southwest wind prevented a wider fire.

Pickmore's December 11 report states that repairs to the church had just been completed at an expense of 1,500 pounds. It records the initial apprehension of design, then says there was every reason to believe the fire was accidental. The inhabitants began another church, subscribed 1,200 pounds and requested government aid. The newspaper suspicion and the Governor's later assessment remain separate.

## Governor's arrival and departure signals

The September 10 *Royal Gazette* reports that HMS *Hydra* arrived carrying Governor Francis Pickmore. The ship fired a 17-gun salute while Pickmore left for Government Wharf. Fort Townshend fired a salute after he landed. The road from the wharf to the fort was lined with military personnel.

The December 3 *Royal Gazette* reports that a Fort Townshend salute announced Pickmore's departure from the wharf for HMS *Tiber*. The ship returned the salute after he arrived aboard.

## Fort and ship salutes

The September 25 *Newfoundland Mercantile Journal* reports that Fort Townshend and the warships in port fired salutes for the anniversary of the sovereign's accession.

The October 26 issue reports that Fort Townshend and the warships in port fired salutes for the coronation anniversary.

## Outer buoy record

Physical page 3 of the *Royal Gazette* file archived as October 15 prints the date October 8. Its local report states that the schooner *Success* passed the outer buoy before a wind shift drove it onto the south point of the harbour after its anchor failed to hold. The atlas preserves the archive filename and printed-page date separately.

## Fort fuel allocation

A Commissariat notice dated November 2 requested 250 cords of firewood for His Majesty's forces. It allocated 100 cords to Fort Townshend, 100 cords to Fort William and 50 cords to Signal Hill. Delivery to the Commissariat fuel yards was required between June 1 and July 15, 1817. Offers could cover all or part of the supply, but not less than 50 cords.

## Local fire alarms

The December 11 *Mercantile Journal* reports that Doctor Carson's family discovered a house fire at about 5:00 a.m., gave the alarm and obtained prompt assistance before material damage occurred. It also reports an earlier fire near Bayly's Auction Mart. Neighbours saw the fire from the street, entered the building, extinguished burning shavings and rescued two sleeping men.

## Source gap

Pickmore's November 1 forwarding letter for the Commanding Engineer's 1816 report survives on physical PDF page 34 of the dispatch volume. The attached report is absent from the reproduced volume. No 1816 fort condition claim is taken from that letter.

## Newspaper and manuscript coverage

The review covered all 31 surviving 1816 files listed in the MUN DAI *Newfoundland Mercantile Journal* collection and all 25 listed *Royal Gazette* files. Together they comprise 222 physical PDF pages. The Mercantile files run from September 11 to December 28. The Gazette files comprise January 4, January 11 and July 16 through December 31. The surviving files do not form a continuous full-year newspaper run.

The official manuscript review covered all 115 physical pages of *Despatches from the Governor of Newfoundland to the Secretary of State for the Colonies, vol. 3, 1816-1817*. The 1816 pages include the St. John's fire reports, relief administration and the December 11 annual report.
