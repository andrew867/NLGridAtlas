# 1848 System Record

## Gisborne lecture in Saint John, New Brunswick

On April 13, the *Morning Courier* reprinted a Saint John, New Brunswick report of a lecture by F. N. Gisborne at the Mechanics' Institute. The report identified Gisborne as superintendent of the British North American Electric Telegraph Association at Quebec.

Gisborne explained the battery, galvanic action and the use of the earth as a return conductor. His working model used an electromagnetically actuated lever to mark a moving strip of paper. The article also described station instruments and line-maintenance arrangements.

The lecture occurred in Saint John, New Brunswick. It is the first contemporary source located in this queue that identifies Gisborne's telegraph work before the 1849 Newfoundland proposals.

## Mainland route toward Halifax

On June 13, the *Morning Courier* reprinted a New Brunswick report on the proposed mainland route. The article estimated the New Brunswick line at £10,000. Boston promoters had agreed to take two-thirds of the stock, leaving one-third to be subscribed in New Brunswick.

The report said Nova Scotia had authorized a line from Halifax to the provincial boundary near Amherst. It also said the Portland company had arranged an extension through Bangor toward Calais. New Brunswick remained the missing link between those works.

These reports establish the adjacent mainland network context. They do not record a Newfoundland line, company, station or operating circuit in 1848.
