# 1829 System Record

## Conception Bay packet communication

The January 1 *Newfoundlander* prints two packet notices on physical PDF p. 1.

The *Express* notice states that the boat had undergone a thorough repair. It continued between Harbour Grace and Portugal Cove. It left Harbour Grace at 09:00 on Monday, Wednesday and Friday, and left Portugal Cove at noon on the next day. A letter carrier left St. John's at 08:00. Sundays and bad weather were excepted. The published fares were 10 shillings for a cabin passenger, 5 shillings for a steerage passenger and 6 pence for a single letter. T. Ridley at Harbour Grace and James Clift at St. John's were the agents.

James Doyle's *Dart* was described as newly fitted and safe. The notice states that it would continue between Carbonear and Portugal Cove for the remainder of the season. It left Carbonear at 09:00 on Monday and Thursday. The St. John's connection operated on Tuesday evening and Saturday morning. Doyle stated that he would deliver letters and parcels personally.

The March 26 *Newfoundlander* prints the winter arrangement on physical PDF p. 1. The *Express* was laid up for the winter. A suitable boat with an experienced crew would run between Harbour Grace and Portugal Cove when favourable opportunities occurred. If water communication stopped, a land letter carrier would leave Harbour Grace weekly, weather permitting. This notice records a fallback between water and land carriage. It does not identify the date on which the packet service began.

The June 18 *Newfoundlander* prints three operating notices on physical PDF p. 1. The *Express* and *Dart* notices retain their routes. Matthew Guswell stated that he had launched a safe and commodious packet boat built expressly to carry letters and passengers. It left Portugal Cove at 11:00 for Carbonear on Monday, Cubits on Wednesday and Harbour Grace on Friday. It left each destination at 08:00 on the next day, subject to wind and weather. The published fares were 10 shillings for ladies and gentlemen, 5 shillings for other passengers and 6 pence for a letter.

These notices document operating schedules and service arrangements. They do not establish when the *Express* or *Dart* first entered service.

## Lightning damage report

The August 25 *Royal Gazette* reports a storm at St. John's and Carbonear on physical PDF pp. 2 to 3.

The article states that thunder, lightning and heavy rain affected St. John's and the surrounding area on the previous Friday. It reports that lightning struck the frame of a newly roofed house owned by John Currie on elevated ground north of the town. The account describes split uprights, displaced roof members and fragments thrown from the structure.

The same article reports damage at Carbonear. It states that lightning entered the chimney of a rigging loft attached to a beach store owned by Gosse, Pack and Fryer, near an iron plate. The account describes damaged brickwork, scorched and split beams, and an opening in the clapboard at the end of the store.

The newspaper used the period term *electric fluid*. This is a contemporary damage report, not an engineering investigation of the discharge path.

## Newspaper search

The review covered 193 surviving 1829 newspaper files: 51 issues of *The Newfoundlander*, 49 issues of *The Royal Gazette* and 93 issues of the *Public Ledger*. The MUN issue inventory marks the October 22 *Newfoundlander* as missing and states that the December 31 issue was not published.

The *Public Ledger* corroborates the winter *Express* arrangement and Matthew Guswell's later packet notice. It did not add a distinct local route to the three notices selected above. No named 1829 Newfoundland lighthouse project, electrical telegraph, installed electrical apparatus or operating electrical circuit was located in the surviving files. Foreign scientific reports and advertisements were not treated as Newfoundland equipment records.
