# 1811 System Record

## Fort Amherst light-house

A notice dated April 4 states that a light-house had been established at Fort Amherst on the south side of the entrance to St. John's Harbour. The committee published an operating season from March 15 through December 31 inclusive.

## Fishing-room land and harbour operation

A proclamation dated September 26 states that the 1811 Act ended public use of the Admirals Room or King's Beach and rooms westward. Land at the western extremity of the harbour had been surveyed and judged better suited to drying, curing and husbanding fish. Boundary marks were erected at its east and west limits, and the land was declared a Fishing Ships' Room for the current fishing season. The record concerns surveyed fishery land, not a government-built wharf.

Two proclamations dated October 12 protected access to Ship's Coves as a fire precaution and established charges for merchant vessels using harbour buoys laid down for His Majesty's ships. The fire-access rules required immediate removal of obstructions and allowed later materials to be seized and auctioned. The buoy charges distinguished vessels below and above 100 tons; receipts went to a charitable fund.

## Hospital proposals

A Grand Jury committee notice dated October 14 sought one penny in the pound from servants' wages for a hospital intended for sick persons. The governor's November 4 report records subscriptions, a small district assessment and land set apart for a public hospital. It says the work was to begin immediately. Neither record establishes completion in 1811.

## Ordnance fuel and Powder House

An October 24 Ordnance tender sought 300 pounds of dipped English candles and 100 cords of best firewood for Fort Townshend or the Ordnance Fuel Yard. A separate notice dated October 31 states that people had fixed marks to the Powder House on the Barrens and damaged the structure by firing at them. The notice says the damage had been repaired.

## Portugal Cove road

Notices dated October 31 opened subscriptions for a good road from St. John's to Portugal Cove and invited proposals for construction no less than 14 feet wide. Stephen Knight and John Dunscomb received subscriptions. The source records a proposed road contract, not completed construction.

## Churches, gaol, Fire Society and fishery returns

The governor's November 4 report records work or funding at St. John's, Trinity, Greenspond, Burin, Fortune Bay, Brigus and Morton's Harbour. It states that Burin gaol was nearly complete. It records £60 paid to the Fire Society and £20 paid to P. W. Carter for accurate fishery returns. The status of each church project is retained from the report; requests for further government contributions are not treated as completed expenditure.

A November 6 letter proposed a separate small gaol for debtors because the common gaol was the only secure place and was reported unfit for that use.

## Town and harbour improvement petition

A meeting at Merchants' Hall on November 7 petitioned to use ship-room lease revenues for St. John's town and harbour improvements. The petition proposed paving, lighting and widening streets, a market place, a seminary, poor relief and a police board. Its figures of 10,000 inhabitants, more than 500 sail entering during the season and nearly £2,000 in annual rent income are reported petition figures, not independent measurements.

## Public administration and Government House

A November 8 letter described a cash shortage in public departments and proposed an annual £10,000 supply in small notes, tokens or coins. A November 9 letter requested additional furniture for Government House and states that repairs and alterations had been underway during the year.
