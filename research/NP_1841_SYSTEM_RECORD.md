# 1841 System Record

## Admiralty mail packets

On January 21, the *Newfoundlander* printed an Admiralty contract notice for the fast-sailing British-built vessels *Sandwich* and *Charles Buchan*. The notice said that the vessels would carry Her Majesty's mails between St. John's and Halifax.

Departure from Halifax was to follow the arrival of the mail steamers from England. Each packet was to remain at St. John's for no more than forty-eight hours. The notice defines the contracted route and operating rule. It does not identify a completed voyage by either vessel.

## Electromagnetic sounding apparatus

On June 10, the *Newfoundlander* printed a report on Bain's electromagnetic sea-sounding apparatus. It described an electromagnet, galvanic battery, insulated wires, iron armature, hammer and bell. The insulated wires also formed the line to the sounding weight. A circuit change when the weight reached the seabed released the hammer to strike the bell.

The apparatus was described for local readers. The article does not place the device aboard a Newfoundland vessel.

## Magnetic printing telegraph

On October 20, the *Times and General Commercial Gazette* printed a report on Bain's magnetic printing telegraph at the Polytechnic Institution. The article compared the apparatus with older signalling telegraphs and the magnetic telegraph on the Great Western Railway. It reported that Bain's system printed messages rapidly. It contrasted the apparatus with older telegraphs that it described as useless in darkness or fog.

The apparatus and the operating railway telegraph were outside Newfoundland. The report records locally published telecommunications knowledge, not a Newfoundland line.

## Proposed St. John's gas-light company

On November 9, the *Royal Gazette* printed a letter from a person signing as one of the subscribers. The writer reported that a meeting held eight or nine months earlier had supported the establishment of a gas-light company in St. John's. The meeting resolved to request English estimates and technical information about gas lighting.

The writer said that reports from scientific persons had arrived, but that the subscribers had not received them and no further step had been taken. The letter asked the committee to determine whether the subscribers would proceed. This is evidence of a local proposal at the estimate stage, not construction or operation.

No named 1841 Newfoundland electric-telegraph line, company, station, construction project or operating circuit was located.
