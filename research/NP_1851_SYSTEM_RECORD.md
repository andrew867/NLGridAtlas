# 1851 System Record

## Company act

Chapter 7, passed May 31, incorporated the Newfoundland Electric Telegraph Company. Authorized capital was £20,000 in 1,000 shares of £20. The company could increase capital by no more than £10,000.

The act authorized a trunk from St. John's toward Cape Ray. It authorized a branch from St. John's toward Trepassey or another point on the southwestern coast and branches to towns and villages. These routes could not interfere with the St. John's and Carbonear Electric Telegraph Company lines. The act also authorized a survey and submarine extension from Cape Ray to Cape North, Cape Breton, with a connection to Nova Scotia.

The company could place works along highways and enter land. It could take posts and building materials from public land. Arbitration governed compensation for private loss. Government messages had priority at rates no higher than private traffic.

The act set a £5 to £10 penalty for interrupting messages. Wilful and malicious damage to lines, works, buildings or machinery was a misdemeanor. The company had to submit detailed annual accounts.

After half the stock was subscribed, construction had started and 150 miles had been completed, the government could grant alternating half-mile parcels on both sides of the line. Settlement conditions required one settler per 100 acres within five years.

The government could pay six per cent interest on company expenditure until the St. John's to Cape North route was complete. The total was capped at £1,800, with annual limits of £300, £600 and £900. The government could take stock for its payments. The act also granted £750 for the survey and admitted telegraph wire and apparatus free of duty.

The act became void unless half the capital was subscribed and effective operations began within two years. After 30 years, the government could acquire the line, branches, batteries, posts, wires and machinery with twelve months' notice. Compensation was actual outlay plus 50 per cent, provided shareholders had realized at least six per cent interest.

## Conception Bay construction

On May 6, F. N. Gisborne reported that the St. John's and Carbonear company stock had been taken up and that construction pickets had been purchased. His prediction of operation within four months was a forecast.

A June 23 Secretary's Office notice appointed six commissioners under Chapter 7: the Attorney General, Joseph Noad, Charles F. Bennett, the Speaker of the House of Assembly, Hugh W. Hoyles and Ambrose Shea.

The September 16 *Morning Post* reported poles planted as far as Brigus while the wire was still expected. On October 2, it reprinted a Harbor Grace report that wire had arrived on the *Victoria* for Ridley and Sons. The article described wire about one-eighth inch thick and said almost all poles had been erected.

The November 21 *Public Ledger* reported that some telegraph posts had been cut down near the head of Conception Bay. The report gives no incident date, damaged-post count or repair date.

On December 2, a Harbor Grace report claimed the St. John's to Carbonear line was complete. The *Public Ledger* immediately disputed the report and stated that the wires in that district extended no farther toward St. John's than Topsail. On December 9, the paper still described the connection as very nearly complete and reported that wire had reached the western edge of St. John's. Neither report establishes public operation.

## Cape Ray route survey

The September 3 *Morning Courier* reported that Gisborne and a party of six left St. John's at 6 a.m. Each carried a 70 lb knapsack with three weeks of provisions.

Gisborne's surviving second journal covers October 7 to December 4. The first 100-page volume has not been located. The journal assigns road estimates commonly between £25 and £30 per mile to surveyed sections. It records separate bridge or stream-crossing estimates from £10 to £80. These figures are field estimates, not final construction costs.

On November 14, Gisborne recorded Cape Ray about 13 or 14 miles away. Snow prevented the party from proceeding. The journal states that a road could be made between Burnt Island Brook and the cape. The return journey reached Salmonier on December 2, Holyrood on December 3 and St. John's on December 4.

The December 9 *Public Ledger* reported that Gisborne considered the route practicable and estimated the work at about £40,000 in current money. It also reported an American offer to supply wire for a little more than £19,000 and take £2,500 as an interest in the scheme. These are reported estimates and financing terms, not completed transactions.
