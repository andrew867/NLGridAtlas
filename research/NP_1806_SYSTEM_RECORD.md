# 1806 System Record

## St. John's road rights and fire access

In an April 19 despatch, Erasmus Gower described a road opened in 1804 behind the fishery, 200 yards from high-water mark. House building had been allowed on the upper side, but several occupiers had not opened the portions crossing their ground. Gower deferred removal of those obstructions while he sought a legal opinion on the Crown's authority over land held by long possession or earlier governors' grants.

The same despatch described a former fishery cook room converted to a retail shop. It projected into the lower or main street and left a crooked passage only six or seven feet wide. Gower reported that carts could not pass conveniently and that fire engines could not move through the street between the two parts of town. He proposed removing enough of the building to provide a sufficient passage, subject to legal advice. The despatch does not establish that removal occurred.

## Currency for garrison payments

Gower recommended another specie shipment for garrison payments after reporting that the previous year's shipment had benefited the public service. He proposed £7,000 in Spanish dollars and £500 in copper two-penny and penny coins. The April 19 despatch records a recommendation, not a confirmed shipment.

## Ordnance Wharf and land controls

From HMS *Isis* at Spithead on June 18, Gower reported that the Ordnance Wharf at St. John's required action. He proposed that future repairs be divided equally between the Ordnance and contingent departments. Without use of the wharf, he expected that private wharf hire would be required for landing garrison supplies at considerable expense.

Gower also intended to remove a fence around the Ordnance limekiln on the Barrens and a wharf extension over ground adjoining the Ordnance Wharf. He intended to require the Signal Hill Powder Shed to be returned to the Ordnance Department and to prevent fires on the Barrens that could endanger it. These were stated intentions, not completed removals.

## Powder House clearances

Gower reported measurements for two houses near the Powder House. The Chief Justice's double house was about 240 yards away. The Customs Surveyor's dwelling was 365 yards away and had been reported safe by the Ordnance Storekeeper. These are reported 1806 measurements, not a modern survey.

## Government House outbuildings

In an August 18 despatch from Fort Townshend, Gower reported that the Government House outbuildings had decayed. A replacement plan was estimated at £100. He directed the estimate to be followed and the work to be put into execution, but the supporting vouchers were not accepted without formal authority. He therefore requested accounting authorization. The despatch establishes directed work and an unresolved payment authority, not a completion date.

## Fort Townshend governor's garden

Gower reported that the road described in his November 16, 1804 letter had taken a considerable part of the Governor's Garden. A replacement garden near Fort Townshend required clearing and fencing. He authorized £41 17s for the labour and requested approval of the expenditure.
