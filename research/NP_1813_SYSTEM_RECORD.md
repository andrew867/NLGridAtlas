# 1813 System Record

## Ordnance fuel and oil

An Ordnance Barrack Office notice dated January 28 sought 3,000 hogsheads of English or Sydney coal, 200 cords of firewood, 150 gallons of cod-fish oil and 100 gallons of seal oil. The notice invited proposals for supply.

## Bay Bulls road shelter

A General Quarter Sessions notice dated January 29 required the trees within 40 yards of each side of the new road from St. John's to Bay Bulls to remain as a screen from the weather. It prohibited cutting or destroying trees inside that distance.

## Newfoundland Hospital

The foundation stone of the Newfoundland Hospital was laid on June 4 by Governor Richard Goodwin Keats. The June 10 report gives the procession from the Court House to the hospital ground and the inscription placed with the stone.

A proclamation dated July 13 states that considerable progress had been made on the hospital building. It confirmed the assessment that funded the institution with voluntary subscriptions.

## Garrison fuel and truckage

A June 10 Commissariat notice sought 3,000 hogsheads of English or Sydney coal for King's Wharf by September 15 and 300 cords of firewood divided equally among the fuel yards at Signal Hill, Fort Townshend and Fort William by October 15.

A truckage notice dated June 24 requested separate rates for coal from King's Wharf to each fort, firewood to each fort, firewood between Fort William and Signal Hill, provisions from King's Wharf to each fort, and provisions between Fort William and Signal Hill. The proposed contract ran for 12 months.

## St. John's land returns

A proclamation dated June 17 required property returns for land outside the town. It defined the area with the road from Quidi Vidi to Fort Townshend over King's Bridge, the road from Fort Townshend to Monday's Pond by Best's Farm, land within four miles of Riverhead, and land on the south side of the harbour more than 200 yards from the water. It requested a plan or sketch where practical.

## Fire Engine No. 1

An August 5 notice asked anyone with demands against Fire Engine No. 1 to send them to Richard Langley, Captain. The notice establishes the named engine organization and its accounting contact. It does not identify the engine's owner, builder or commissioning date.

## Government House

Governor Keats reported on July 29 that the Assistant Commissary General had orders to complete Government House. The order did not include the fences or outbuildings. Keats requested authority to complete them with the house.

## Record Office proposal

Keats reported on September 25 that government records were kept loosely in the Secretary's Office or Court House and were exposed to fire. He stated that records before 1748 had been destroyed by fire. He proposed a small fire-secure Record Office at Government House and enclosed an estimate. The letter does not establish construction.

## Public accounts and local works

Keats's November 10 account records a completed payment toward a subscription for a church at Morton's Harbour, a survey of ship rooms in the Trinity district, and repairs and painting at the St. John's Court House.

The same letter states that the Trinity jail and Court House were in a forward state. Keats requested 300 pounds to complete those buildings and begin church repairs. It also records authorized payments of 400 pounds for Fortune Bay Church and 80 pounds for Trinity Church that remained unpaid until their conditions were met.

## Fortification and regional defence report

Keats's December 16 annual report states that a small field work had been established on the road to Petty Harbour and that batteries on the south side of the entrance to St. John's harbour had been completed.

The report states that Signal Hill and its dependencies remained nearly unchanged and unfinished. The sea-face platform at Fort Townshend was crumbling. Keats reported doubts about the cost of the proposed Signal Hill plan and recommended immediate work at Fort Townshend and on its communication route to Signal Hill before the larger project.

The report also states that artillery and ammunition had been sent to Bay Bulls, Fermeuse, Trepassey and Placentia. It identifies Bay Bulls as a place where supplies and stores gathered when weather or coastal ice delayed entry to St. John's.
