# 1860 System Record

## Company management

The 1860 *Newfoundland Almanack* lists the New York, Newfoundland, and London Telegraph Company. Peter Cooper was president, Cyrus W. Field was vice-president, Moses Taylor was treasurer and David D. Field was counsel. Nicholas Stabb was the general agent for Newfoundland.

## Cape Race interception service

A January 6 *Morning Post* advertisement states that the Telegraphic News Agency transmitted messages between Europe and America over the line between St. John's and New York. It reports a vessel stationed off Cape Race to intercept European steamers. Messages obtained from those vessels were telegraphed from Newfoundland.

The August 25 *Morning Post* prints a dispatch from the St. John's office of the New York, Newfoundland, and London Telegraph Company. The dispatch states that the *City of Baltimore* was intercepted off Cape Race at 7 p.m. on August 23. The transmitted news included Liverpool and Queenstown dates.

## Harbor Grace line damage

The November 2 *St. John's Daily News* prints a dispatch dated November 1. The dispatch reports that a mob began tearing down the Harbor Grace telegraph line at about 7:30 p.m. on the previous evening. It does not identify the damaged length, equipment or return-to-service time.

## Carbonear office loss

The November 8 *St. John's Daily News* prints a report dated November 7 concerning the Carbonear fire. The list of destroyed premises includes the telegraph office. The report does not identify its equipment or replacement arrangement.

## Wycocomagh line cutting

The December 12 *Morning Post* reports that two men had recently been convicted in Cape Breton for cutting lines of the Newfoundland company. Each received a fine of two shillings sterling or 48 hours' imprisonment. The article states that lines near Wycocomagh were being cut repeatedly and that the interruptions caused losses to Newfoundland's mercantile community. It does not identify the incident dates, damaged lengths or repair times.
