# Newfoundland Power 1996 system record

## Primary records

- Newfoundland Power, *1996 Annual Report and Regulatory Returns*, `CA-213A.pdf`, physical PDF pp. 8 to 9, 11, 21, 29, 38, 49 and 53.
- Newfoundland Power, `CA-446Rev.pdf`, physical PDF p. 1.

## Reliability and network work

The revised reliability response reports 1996 SAIFI of 2.66 interruptions per customer and SAIDI of 3.87 hours per customer, excluding Newfoundland and Labrador Hydro loss-of-supply outages.

The annual report states that the transmission and substation insulator program began in 1989 and was scheduled through 2001. It covered more than 70,000 insulators dating from the 1960s and 1970s, at a reported cost above $10 million. Insulator failures contributed to 1996 outages on the Bonavista and Burin peninsulas and parts of the Avalon Peninsula. High winds and salt spray caused additional December outages around Port aux Basques and the Port au Port Peninsula.

Physical PDF p. 11 contains a dated service-area and transmission-lines map. It distinguishes Newfoundland Power, Newfoundland and Labrador Hydro and Deer Lake Power facilities and major service centres.

## Telecommunications

The annual report states that an improved telecommunications system was substantially completed in early 1997. It improved radio and substation-control capabilities and supported workflow software. This is an early-1997 completion statement, not a 1996 commissioning date.

## Rose Blanche development plan

The 1996 plan described Rose Blanche as a 5.5 MW project producing 22 GWh annually. Construction was planned to begin in 1997, with completion in fall 1998. The filing projected annual displacement of 36,000 barrels of oil, lower southwest transmission losses and improved supply reliability. Later annual reports use a 6.1 MW rating, so the 5.5 MW value remains attached to the dated 1996 proposal.

## Capital, energy and assets

Return 4A, physical PDF p. 38, records $28.770 million actual capital expenditure. It includes $2.061 million for energy supply, $1.179 million for substations, $1.634 million for transmission, $11.448 million for distribution and $0.930 million for telecommunications.

The ten-year summary, physical PDF p. 29, reports 423 million kWh generated and 4,236 million kWh purchased. Return 13, physical PDF p. 53, records 220,675 MWh of normalized losses, equal to 4.7 percent of purchased and produced energy.

At December 31, substations had $47.189 million net book value from $87.130 million cost, $40.010 million accumulated depreciation and $0.069 million construction in progress. Transmission had $44.005 million net book value from $70.725 million cost, $27.330 million accumulated depreciation and $0.610 million construction in progress.

## Unlocated 1996 measures

The reviewed records do not publish a 1996 service-availability percentage, operating-area reliability table, peak demand or system load factor.
