# 1842 System Record

## Galvanic rock blasting

On June 14, the *Public Ledger* printed a technical account of blasting at Wester Craigs Quarry. The procedure used a battery and connecting wires. The workmen placed charges in the rock, ran wires from the charges to the main coil and used the battery to fire the charges simultaneously.

The work occurred outside Newfoundland. It records electrical-engineering information available to local readers, not local plant or construction.

## Steam communication at St. John's

The August 9 *Royal Gazette* reported that the steamship *John M'Adam* had arrived at St. John's from Liverpool by way of Cork and Halifax. The report rated the vessel at 120 horsepower and 220 tons. It attributed the long crossing to poor coal.

The same page reported that *St. George* arrived from Cork in fourteen days while bound for Charlottetown. The vessel was to carry mails between Prince Edward Island, Miramichi and Pictou. The newspaper also announced a proposed *John M'Adam* excursion calling at Brigus, Harbor Grace, Carbonear and Trinity. The notice is evidence of the planned calls, not evidence that the excursion was completed.

A letter on the next page proposed monthly steam-mail service between St. John's and Halifax. It printed an annual sailing-mail allowance of £4,150, a £1,000 Newfoundland annual subsidy and a £500 Nova Scotia annual subsidy. The writer identified a 130-horsepower, 270-ton vessel then in St. John's harbour as suitable for the route. The route remained a proposal in this letter.

## Acoustic submarine telegraph

The April 26 *Royal Gazette* printed an article titled "The Submarine Telegraph." It described Colladon's Lake Geneva experiments with bells and underwater sound. The proposed signalling method used sound received near the water surface for navigation and fog signalling. It did not use an electric circuit or submarine cable.

No named 1842 Newfoundland electric-telegraph line, company, station, construction project or operating circuit was located.
