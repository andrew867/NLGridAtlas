## Feeder additions for load growth

The filed program included RRD-10, SPF-02, PBD-01, PUL-05, CHA-04, and three feeder exits at the proposed Airport Substation. The approved program estimate was $2.655 million, physical PDF p. 186.

RRD-10 served approximately 1,276 residential and commercial customers. The filed work would replace non-standard 2/0 ACSR with 4/0 AASC on 3.8 km of three-phase line along Portugal Cove Road. The line would carry planned transfers from Broad Cove through RRD-10 to the new Airport Substation feeders. The allocation was $654,000, physical PDF pp. 181 to 182.

SPF-02 was a 12.5 kV feeder serving approximately 1,421 primarily residential customers in Makinsons, Cupids, Brigus, and Roaches Line. The 4.0 km section had 1970s-vintage #6 copper and mixed two-phase and three-phase construction. The measured load was approximately 127 A per phase on the two-phase section. The filed work would convert 3.2 km from two phase to three phase and reconductor the full 4.0 km with 4/0 AASC. The allocation was $600,000, physical PDF pp. 182 to 183.

PUL-05 supplied a Middle Cove Road single-phase section. The filing records 118 customers in 2010, 134 in the filed current record, a 95 A 2020 forecast, and non-standard #4 copper. The proposed work would convert 2.0 km from single phase to three phase. The allocation was $360,000, physical PDF p. 185.

CHA-04 supplied a single-phase section on Three Island Pond Road. The filing records 128 customers in 2010, 163 in the filed current record, and a 94 A 2020 forecast. The proposed work would convert 1.3 km from single phase to three phase. The allocation was $290,000, physical PDF p. 185.

The expenditure report records $1.899 million of 2021 expenditure and $671,000 carried into 2022 for the load-growth feeder program. The report does not identify the feeder associated with the carryover, physical PDF pp. 4 and 10. The station records therefore retain the four scopes as proposed work.

## Substations and transmission

The filed 2021 station program included $1.845 million for Dunville, $3.124 million for Rattling Brook, and $184,000 for monitoring upgrades, physical PDF p. 107. The load-growth study selected replacement of the 8.3 MVA DUN-T1 with the former 25 MVA LEW-T1 spare and construction of the new 25 MVA, 66/12.5 kV Airport Substation, physical PDF pp. 118 to 125.

The transmission program included 30.0 km of 124L, built in 1964, and 2.8 km of new 136L and 137L extensions, physical PDF p. 147. The expenditure report records $6.648 million against $6.170 million approved for transmission-line rebuilds. It records $106,000 of 2021 expenditure and $1.237 million carried into 2022 for the 35L extension, physical PDF p. 9.

## Equipment failures and communications

The in-service failure program recorded $4.113 million against $3.413 million approved. Newfoundland Power reported increased failures of high-voltage disconnect switches, power transformers, tap changers, and portable-substation equipment. Failed-equipment material cost was $2.731 million in 2021, compared with a 2016 to 2020 average of $2.248 million, physical PDF p. 19.

The fibre-optic cable program recorded $238,000 of 2021 expenditure and $112,000 carried into 2022, physical PDF p. 13.

## Financial report check

The annual report records 2021 plant additions of $11.580 million for substations, $11.446 million for transmission, $52.925 million for distribution, and $235,000 for communications, physical PDF p. 52. Its capital-expenditure table repeats the approved and actual category totals from the expenditure report, physical PDF p. 53. It does not add station topology, feeder scope, equipment ratings, or asset-level commissioning dates.
