# 1854 System Record

## Telegraph company reorganization

Chapter 1, passed April 15, accepted the Newfoundland Electric Telegraph Company's surrendered charter and repealed its incorporating acts. Chapter 2 incorporated the New York, Newfoundland and London Telegraph Company. It set initial capital at £312,500, or $1.5 million.

The act required trunk lines from St. John's toward Cape Ray and from St. John's toward Trepassey. It preserved the existing St. John's and Carbonear Electric Telegraph Company line. It granted a conditional fifty-year exclusive right, with the mainland connection required within five years. The government guaranteed twenty years of interest on fifty £1,000 bonds. The earlier company's lines, wires, posts, insulators, cables and other property could transfer to the new company.

## Inland construction

On June 3, the *Times* reported that the *Victoria* had carried its first group of workers to begin operations on the inland line.

An August 19 correspondent reported that the Telegraph Company had completed its road as far as the Peak. The remaining route had been opened, with some portions cleared and drained. Five or six miles still required drainage and gravel before the road reached Long Harbour.

## October work notice

Chandler White's October 7 notice requested proposals for unfinished bridle roads and bridges between Cape Ray and St. John's, roads and bridges from Renews to Trepassey, and the poles and braces required for both lines.

## Reported mainland route change

A November 1 article reprinted an exchange report that abandoned the proposed Newfoundland to Prince Edward Island submarine connection. It prints the replacement route as Cape Race to Nova Scotia at or near Cape North. The April statute and later construction records use Cape Ray. The conflicting endpoint is retained as printed and is not assigned to the network map.

## St. John's to Carbonear line condition

By December 20, the St. John's and Carbonear Electric Telegraph Company had appointed Hartley J. Gisborne as superintendent. After inspecting the line, he reported it in very bad condition and found that the wire had been deliberately cut in some places.
