# 1856 System Record

## Inland line construction

An August 19 report states that the wires were complete to Long Harbour in Fortune Bay and lacked about six miles of being complete to Bay Despair. It places the gap midway between Long Harbour and Conn River. The *Victoria* had returned after visiting the line.

A separate September report prints only `Pond` for its last construction endpoint. That incomplete place name is not assigned to the network map.

## St. John's to Bay Despair messages

On September 23, the *Morning Post* reported that messages had passed between St. John's and Bay Despair. Telegraph Company superintendent Mr. Simpson sent a message from Bay Despair reporting the *Victoria*'s arrival and intended departure for White Bear Bay. The article described completion to Port Hood as probable, not completed.

## Cape Breton submarine cable

On September 24, the *Telegraph* reported that the *Propontis* had returned from Newfoundland after successfully laying submarine cables between Cape Breton and Newfoundland and between New Brunswick and Prince Edward Island. The article does not state the cable-laying dates or landing points.

## Baddeck business message

An October 9 report records a business message from Baddeck, Cape Breton, to William Pitts in St. John's. An answer was promptly sent back over the wires. The newspaper calls it the first intimation that the telegraph in Newfoundland had been completed. This exchange predates the company's formal public-opening notice.

## Bay Despair to Port aux Basques interruption

An October 30 report states that an interruption between Bay Despair and Port aux Basques had prevented messages between Port aux Basques and St. John's since the preceding Saturday morning. It does not identify the fault, exact incident date or repair time.

## Public opening

The company's November 14 notice states that the telegraph line connecting Newfoundland with Cape Breton was complete and open to the public. It offered message transmission between the colony and the United States and British North America. The St. John's office listed service from 9 a.m. to 1 p.m., 2 p.m. to 5 p.m., and 7 p.m. to 8 p.m. A. Shea was local manager.
