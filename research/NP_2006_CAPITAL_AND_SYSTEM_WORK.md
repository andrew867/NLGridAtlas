## 123L

The 123L rehabilitation report is on physical PDF pp. 332 to 338.

- 138 kV between Clarenville and Catalina, through Princeton Pond.
- 94 km of wood H-frame construction, built in 1976.
- Approximately 5,500 customers were supplied on the Bonavista Peninsula.
- The filing identified limited contingency supply through 110L.
- Heavy ice collapsed approximately 4.3 km in 1992.
- Hardware had been replaced on approximately 30 km before the 2006 filing. About 40 percent of defective insulators had also been replaced.
- The filing proposed hardware and insulator replacement on the remaining 56 km for $372,000.

The filed route map is preserved as `public/reference-maps/123l-2006-route-338.png`.

## Distribution reliability studies

The five feeder reports are on physical PDF pp. 405 to 451.

### LEW-02

- 25 kV, approximately 1,550 customers, with original construction dating from 1964.
- 185 interruption reports and 3,071,925 customer outage minutes in the study period.
- Conductor failure accounted for 44 percent of the outage minutes. Weather accounted for 42 percent.
- The filing proposed conductor and insulator replacement, exposed-section relocation, loading work, and a remote recloser.
- Filed program: $888,000, comprising $432,000 in 2006 and $456,000 in 2007.

The 2010 capital application states that substantial work was completed in 2006, later stages were deferred, and the remaining 2009 allocation was cancelled after a revised reliability analysis, physical PDF pp. 349 to 353.

### GLV-02

- 25 kV, approximately 1,222 customers, with original construction dating from 1964 and expansion in 1976.
- 151 interruption reports and 1,476,700 customer outage minutes in the study period.
- Conductor failure accounted for 48 percent of the outage minutes.
- Filed program: $1.245 million from 2006 through 2008, including $465,000 in 2006.

The 2010 filing reports $781,000 of work completed in 2008. It states that the remaining 2009 allocation was cancelled after reliability improved, physical PDF pp. 349 to 353.

### BCV-02

- 12.5 kV, approximately 62 km and 1,530 customers, including the Bell Island submarine-cable supply.
- Original construction dated from the early 1930s. Approximately 25 percent was rebuilt after the 1984 ice storm.
- 261 interruption reports and 3,512,882 customer outage minutes in the study period.
- The filing proposed rebuilding approximately 10 km of the main trunk with 1/0 AASC conductor and new poles for $440,000.

The 2010 filing says BCV-02 was rebuilt under the distribution reliability initiative in 2006 and had no reliability issues after 2006, physical PDF p. 360.

### SMV-01

- 25 kV, approximately 1,016 customers and 98 km total.
- The route comprised 15.6 km three phase, 26.1 km two phase, and 56 km single phase.
- 226 interruption reports and 2,414,660 customer outage minutes in the study period.
- Weather accounted for 71 percent of the outage minutes.
- The filing proposed two rebuild sections, a one-kilometre relocation, pole and insulator replacement, for $239,000.

No later completion record was located in this queue.

### GPD-01

- 12.5 kV, approximately 233 customers, built in 1980.
- 1/0 AASC extended to the Greenspond causeway. ACSR crossed the causeway and continued through the community.
- 100 interruption reports and 1,756,284 customer outage minutes in the study period.
- An April 2004 outage lasted 28 hours and 16 minutes.
- The filing proposed ACSR, cutout, and clamp replacement, with 14.4 kV construction and 35 kV line-post insulators in the exposed section, for $102,000.

The 2010 filing says GPD-01 work was completed under the rebuild program in 2005 and reports no reliability issues after that work, physical PDF p. 359. The 2006 proposal and the later 2005 completion statement are retained as separate dated records.

The filed LEW-02, GLV-02, and GPD-01 route maps are preserved in `public/reference-maps/`.

## Humber Valley load plan

The Pasadena and Marble Mountain report is on physical PDF pp. 237 to 245.

- PAS-T1 was rated 10 MVA without forced cooling and 13.3 MVA with fans. The 2004 measured peak was 9.04 MVA and the 2006 forecast was 13.22 MVA.
- MMT-T1 was rated 4 MVA. The 2004 measured peak was 3.10 MVA and the 2006 forecast was 3.34 MVA.
- Both stations were supplied by Deer Lake Power Line 1.
- The resort full-build forecast was 14.5 MVA.
- The selected study alternative was a new Little Rapids station with a 25 MVA on-load-tap-changing transformer and LTR-01.
- Filed revenue-requirement net present values were $2.898 million for Pasadena expansion, $2.746 million for Marble Mountain expansion, and $1.873 million for Little Rapids.

The 2013 Humber Valley filing selected work at Marble Mountain rather than documenting Little Rapids as built. The 2006 Little Rapids result remains a selected study alternative, not an as-built station.

## Substation program

The station program is on physical PDF pp. 202 to 235.

- The fleet included approximately 190 power transformers. About 160 had been installed before 1980, physical PDF pp. 210 to 211.
- Approximately 115 transformers were estimated to have silicon-carbide gap arresters. Arrester failures caused 594,218 customer outage minutes in 2004 and 967,414 in the partial 2005 record, physical PDF pp. 210 to 211.
- The equipment inventory included approximately 400 circuit breakers, 200 reclosers, 220 potential transformers, 340 voltage regulators, 120 battery banks, and 2,500 high-voltage switches, physical PDF pp. 214 to 219.
- The Greenhill 301L and 305L breakers were 1975 bulk-oil units containing more than 12,000 litres of oil each. Three corroded 66 kV potential transformers were also selected for replacement.
- VIR-04 and VIR-06 breakers had 12.5 kA interrupting ratings against an 18.4 kA calculated fault level in the filing.
- Newfoundland Power recorded 64 transformers with on-load tap changers. Forty-seven were at least 20 years old. The filing counted 12 tap-changer failures from January 2002 through December 2004 and three more through May 2005, physical PDF pp. 222 to 223.
- Salt Pond and New Chelsea were selected for 66 kV bus differential protection, physical PDF pp. 228 to 231.
- Riverhead and Trepassey were selected for high-side transformer backup fuses, physical PDF pp. 228 to 231.
- PAS-T1 cooling fans were filed at $68,000, physical PDF p. 234.

## Building load control

The standby generation and load-control reports are on physical PDF pp. 457 to 465.

Newfoundland Power estimated that manual load reduction on December 6, 2004 reduced system peak by 2.5 MW. The filing proposed metering and remote control at ten company buildings through the SCADA system for $143,000. Using the stated $79.68 per kW demand charge, the filing estimated approximately $200,000 per year in avoided demand charges.

The equipment schedule in PUB-NP-063 and PUB-NP-064 records Duffy Place and Clarenville for 2006, Gander and Burin for 2007, Carbonear and Stephenville for 2009, and Grand Falls and Corner Brook without a scheduled year. No later completion record was located in this queue, so the atlas does not present the proposed schedule as installed equipment.
