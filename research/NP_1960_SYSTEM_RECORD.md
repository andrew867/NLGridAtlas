# 1960 System Record

## St. John's stations

### Molloy's Lane

Molloy's Lane Substation was built on Topsail Road in 1960 as a transmission and distribution station. The 2023 filing records 66 kV supplies from Stamp's Lane on 15L and Hardwoods on 19L. Two 25 MVA transformers supplied eight 12.5 kV feeders in the filed arrangement.

The original control building housed 12.5 kV switchgear. A catastrophic switchgear failure in the early 1970s damaged the building foundation, walls and roof. Newfoundland Power removed the switchgear and installed outdoor distribution equipment.

Source: [2023 Capital Budget Application, physical PDF pp. 403 and 408](http://www.pub.nl.ca/applications/2023/NP2023Capital/app/From%20NP%20-%202023%20Capital%20Budget%20Application%20-%202022-06-29.PDF#page=403), SHA-256 `be0143cf9814e8573dcb5cdcdfa7fcd213190a5274902d0c5d60becbbf65dd73`.

### Ridge Road date conflict

The 2027 capital filing states that Ridge Road Substation was constructed in 1960. It describes the station as a 66 kV transmission and 12.5 kV distribution station supplied by 30L from King's Bridge and 32L and 67L from Oxen Pond.

The 2007 capital filing states that the same station was built in 1963. Neither filing explains the difference. The atlas therefore retains both dates as unresolved.

## Transmission and distribution

### 403L Robinsons tap

The Robinsons supply was a 4.0 km, three-phase tap from the 66 kV 403L line between St. George's and Lookout Brook. The tap was built in 1960 with 46 single-pole structures and non-standard 1/0 ACSR conductor. The 2020 filing identifies it as the only supply to approximately 1,300 customers in the Robinsons area.

Source: [2020 Capital Budget Application, physical PDF p. 394](http://www.pub.nl.ca/applications/2020/NP2020Capital/applications/NP%20-%202020%20Capital%20Budget%20Application%20-%202019-07-05.PDF#page=394), SHA-256 `eb12a773e3d81aa1cfc7b88aae339cfdb009a91fedd54088d9252d1f56366b47`.

### Makkovik L1

Makkovik L1 was built in 1960 as a 5.4 km, 4.16 kV three-phase feeder to the Ranger Bight Road pump house. Single-phase taps supplied a communications tower, the airport and the sewage lift station. The 2010 filing identifies L1 as the community's primary supply and records approximately 200 customers.

Source: [2010 Capital Budget Application, Volume II, physical PDF p. 232](http://www.pub.nl.ca/applications/ARCHIVE/2010/NLH2010Capital/files/application/NLH2010ApplicationComplete-VolumeII.pdf#page=232), SHA-256 `0c34a4413d80cc3eaa0585060bef94cce5c83922a77b56e2ad5e5c34c9fb22c9`.

## Hydroelectric generation

### Heart's Content replacement plant

The 2.4 MW Heart's Content plant commissioned in 1960 replaced the original plant commissioned in 1918. The plant history gives a 2,370 kW capacity under about 46.9 m net head and says the English Electric vertical Francis turbine and Bruce Peebles generator were commissioned in 1960. A separate development record prints 1969 for the turbine. The component date remains unresolved. The redeveloped plant was remotely controlled from New Chelsea.

## Holyrood transformer T6

The 2011 condition assessment records T6 as a Canadian General Electric transformer manufactured in 1960. It gives three cooling-stage ratings of 25/33.3/41.7 MVA, ONAN/ONAF/ONAF, a 230/138 kV winding voltage, primary taps of four positive and twelve negative 1.25 percent steps, and Z1 of 5.5 percent. The 2009 oil results included elevated carbon monoxide and power factor, contaminants and oxidation products, and low dielectric breakdown voltage.

The document gives a manufacturing year, not a Holyrood installation or in-service year.

Source: [2011 Capital Budget Application, Volume I, physical PDF pp. 699 to 700](http://www.pub.nl.ca/applications/ARCHIVE/NLH2011Capital/files/application/NLH2011ApplicationComplete-VolumeI.pdf#page=699), SHA-256 `d02cac731457ffa45ea1cf8f93af64626f5323fc23f2430e4d5904fb7292647a`.

## Bishop's Falls diesel shop

The Bishop's Falls diesel shop was installed in 1960 as a 40 by 60 foot pre-engineered steel building. It provided a workshop and storage for diesel-generator maintenance, critical spares and specialized tools. A wood-frame extension added offices, a kitchen and a washroom in the 1980s.

Source: [2023 Capital Budget Application, Volume II, physical PDF pp. 640 to 641](http://www.pub.nl.ca/applications/2023/NLH2023Capital/apps/From%20NLH%20-%202023%20Capital%20Budget%20Application%20-%20Volume%20II%20-%202022-07-13.PDF#page=640), SHA-256 `b4ba7fffa4738034133cb0f8e81a494a22e983535f4f588f5856b37225c25323`.
