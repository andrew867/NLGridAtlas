# 1917 System Search

## Port Union hydroelectric development

Newfoundland Power dates the Port Union development and both original generating units to 1917. The detailed development record identifies two horizontal Francis turbines manufactured by the Pelton Waterwheel Company, General Electric generators and a 21.3 m net head. It prints 500 kW total capacity. A separate plant review prints 511 kW, while the selected-unit table rounds PUN-G1 and PUN-G2 to 0.3 MW each. Each capacity remains attached to its source.

The 2011 flood-rehabilitation report says the belt-driven governors on both units were original to the 1917 construction. Floodwater interfered with their operation, the wicket gates did not close as required, and the report recommended replacement with a programmable-logic-controller system.

Later physical descriptions do not provide one stable powerhouse and penstock record. CA-NP-036 describes a concrete powerhouse and the 1984 replacement penstock as approximately 137 m long and 1,370 mm diameter. The 2017 capital filing describes a wood-frame powerhouse on a concrete foundation and a 118 m long by 1.8 m diameter woodstave penstock. These later descriptions are not treated as a 1917 as-built drawing.

## Cochrane Pond watershed diversion

The Cochrane Pond watershed was diverted from Manuels River as part of the Petty Harbour hydroelectric development in 1917. The later study says the Topsail development was constructed after the diversion. Its model found that rerouting Cochrane Pond flows to Topsail increased annual Topsail production by 1.4 GWh but reduced Petty Harbour production by the same amount before considering additional storage effects.

Source: [CA-NP-003](http://www.pub.nl.ca/applications/ARCHIVE/2013/NP2013Capital/files/rfi/CA-NP-003.pdf), physical PDF p. 280, local file `mirror/applications/ARCHIVE/2013/NP2013Capital/files/rfi/CA-NP-003.pdf`, SHA-256 `051c70791e475706e32c6c7842ae62abbec6bb5ac3ff5bcad1705afde935db99`.

## Bay Bulls Big Pond property

The utility property schedule records a Bay Bulls Big Pond parcel acquired from William Frizzelle on December 28, 1917. The legal description says the eastern boundary was intended to include land that would be flooded if Big Pond were raised to the level of Goose Pond.

Source: [CA-NP-016 Attachment A](http://www.pub.nl.ca/applications/2016/NP2016GRA/files/rfi/CA-NP-016,%20Attachment%20A.pdf), physical PDF p. 133, local file `mirror/applications/2016/NP2016GRA/files/rfi/CA-NP-016, Attachment A.pdf`, SHA-256 `84f2db8eec714793a206a1f12175403aa9390f5f9527eae35a8396e9b745cd60`.
