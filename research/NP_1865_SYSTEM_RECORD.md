# 1865 System Record

## Heart's Content landing preparations

The September 2, 1865 *Morning Chronicle* reprints correspondence from Heart's Content dated July 14 and July 18. The July 14 report states that the telegraph house was almost finished. The carpenter had completed his work, the walls were papered and the carpets were down. Alexander McKay and the Atlantic Telegraph Company's local agent, Mr. Lundy, had hired a nearby house as their dwelling.

The correspondent described the harbour entrance as more than 100 feet deep close to shore and reported soundings suitable for the *Great Eastern*. Heart's Content had been selected after examination of the coves and harbours in Trinity Bay. Fritter's Cove, near Perlican, was identified as another site promoted to the surveyors.

These are preparations and site observations. The report does not establish that the 1865 cable entered service.

## Loss of signals and uncertainty

The August 8 *St. John's Daily News* states that no news of the *Great Eastern* or cable had reached St. John's. It described a cable disaster as a probability, not a confirmed event.

The August 21 issue states that no tidings of the *Great Eastern* had been received. It reports that signals from the Atlantic cable ceased on August 2. The newspaper compared the logs of the *North American* and *Persia*, which recorded moderate weather in the relevant area, and stated that the cause remained a mystery. It described a problem in the cable tanks as an impression gaining ground, not a finding.

## Cable parting and end of the 1865 attempt

The 1866 expedition diary printed in *The Times* identifies the position where the cable parted on August 2, 1865. It states that 1,213 miles had then been paid out. This is a retrospective report printed during the successful 1866 expedition.

The September 11, 1865 *Morning Chronicle* prints Heart's Content correspondence dated August 15. The correspondent states that news received at the telegraph house established that the cable had been severed and that the *Great Eastern*, after repeated recovery attempts, had put back to England. This ended the 1865 attempt, but the cable remained available for the recovery operation completed in 1866.
