# 1837 System Record

## Harbour Grace Island lighthouse

Chapter 8, dated November 18, authorized a further loan of up to £500 to complete the lighthouse on Harbour Grace Island. Interest could not exceed six percent per year. The act referred to an earlier lighthouse statute and retained the existing borrowing and repayment framework.

The act assigned keeper appointment and removal to the Governor. The legislature controlled the keeper's salary, lighting allowance and incidental expenses. Lighthouse purchases required a public notice or advertisement for tenders, with the lowest tender for suitable quality preferred. Accounts and vouchers went to the Treasurer, and the legislature was to receive a detailed annual statement of revenue and expenditure.

The *Royal Gazette* printed two notices on November 21. One stated that the lighthouse had been completed and that a powerful fixed light would be exhibited nightly from that date. A commissioners' notice dated November 13 specified operation from sunset to sunrise beginning on Tuesday evening, November 21. It located the light on Harbour Grace Island in Conception Bay and described its eastward or seaward compass direction from north to southwest.

The statute documents financing and control. The notices document completion and the announced start of nightly fixed-light service.

## Published electric-telegraph context

On November 8, the *Times and General Commercial Gazette* printed a report from Munich about Professor Steinheil. The article said that workers were placing iron rods between elevated points and that Steinheil intended to test a plan attributed to Gauss for electrical correspondence. The proposed system was described as a possible faster replacement for the existing telegraph.

The report concerns a proposed experiment outside Newfoundland. No named 1837 Newfoundland electric-telegraph instrument, company, station or circuit was located in the four local newspaper collections searched.
