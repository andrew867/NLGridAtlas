# 1844 System Record

## Highton experimental telegraph report

On June 18, the *Public Ledger* printed a detailed account of experiments on the London and Birmingham Railway near Rugby. The report identified the system as an invention of the Reverend Mr. Highton.

The experimental wire was ten miles long. One third was insulated with glass. The remaining two thirds were suspended on poles without varnish or other insulation. The work tested power loss with different conductor lengths and insulation methods. The report also described automatic signal registration and claimed operation over 400 or 500 miles without intermediate power.

The experiment occurred in England. It is technical information printed for Newfoundland readers, not a Newfoundland installation.

## Unresolved locality in the copied article

The final paragraph says that Mr. Adams had shown telegraphic experiments during lectures in the previous year at the Collegiate Institution "in this town." The column gives no byline or source locality.

The item discusses a Liverpool to London route. St. John's newspapers also reprinted material about the Liverpool Collegiate Institution. The *Times and General Commercial Gazette* of March 8, 1843 explicitly titled its article "Opening of the Liverpool Collegiate Institution" and identified it as abridged from the *Liverpool Chronicle*.

The words "in this town" therefore cannot be assigned to St. John's. No separate Newfoundland lecture notice was located. The atlas does not create an 1843 Newfoundland demonstration from this phrase.

## Mail transition

The 1844 *Newfoundland Almanac* records that sailing packets still carried mail between Halifax and St. John's. They remained at St. John's for 48 hours before returning with mail for England.

The same page says the steam vessel *North America* was expected to replace the sailing packets beginning in April. This is a proposed service change in the almanac, not completion evidence.

No named 1844 Newfoundland telegraph line, company, station, construction project or operating circuit was located. The atlas timeline entry records the publication of technical information, not local infrastructure.
