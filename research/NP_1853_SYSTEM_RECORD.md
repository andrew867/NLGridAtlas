# 1853 System Record

## Regional submarine cable

The January 25 *Public Ledger* reprinted a *New Brunswicker* report of a telegraphic message sent from Charlottetown to Cape Tormentine on the previous day. The report states that F. N. Gisborne forwarded the message from the Cape.

The January 29 *Courier* reported the Cape Tormentine to Carlton Head submarine cable complete. Captain Kennedy and F. N. Gisborne directed the work. Shallow water kept the steamer one mile from the New Brunswick shore, so the crew brought that mile ashore with a kedge anchor, four horses and oxen. The operation occupied eleven hours. The report states that the completed regional line connected Sackville and Charlottetown.

## Newfoundland route and materials

The February 11 *Public Ledger* reprinted a proposed route from St. John's to Cape Ray. It stated that the line west of Brigus would be underground and described a 139-mile submarine crossing from Cape Ray. The article is a design report, not installation evidence.

On March 26, the *Courier* reported that overland wire had arrived. Subterranean wire, carriages and mules were expected in six weeks. The expected equipment and local hiring remained plans.

## Company capital and line damage

Chapter 11, passed June 15, authorized the Newfoundland Electric Telegraph Company to increase capital from £100,000 to £300,000. It increased the authorized share count from 1,000 to 3,000 at £100 each.

Chapter 16 granted £86 13s 4d to the St. John's and Carbonear Electric Telegraph Company for wire damage and other losses. It directed that the previous session's line-protection grant not be spent.

## Ship Harbour Road construction

The road-commission report published September 20 states that the Cape Ray Telegraph Company used the Ship Harbour Road and had dug trenches for subterranean electric wires beside it. The road had been surveyed, opened, cleared and bridged for 28 miles to Long Harbour. Fourteen miles had been drained and gravelled. The road was about seven feet wide.

## Work suspension and financing claim

P. F. Little's September 21 letter states that telegraph labourers had applied to recover unpaid wages after they were deprived of work during the season. He asked whether the company would pay them, continue the line or abandon completed work. The company's October 25 reply promised payment of validated claims and said work would proceed as early as practicable. It gave no restart date.

In a November 8 letter, F. N. Gisborne stated that English supporters had offered £11,000 against the company's charter and works. He said the offer had revived the New York backers. The letter does not record a completed financing transaction.

The April 27 *Courier* annual report gives March 1852 dates for the first Brigus and Harbor Grace messages. Those operating events belong to the 1852 record.
