# 1808 System Record

## Garrison coal and lighting supply

A June 1 Commissary's Office tender sought 2,500 hogsheads of Sydney or English coal for delivery at the King's Wharf. It also sought 3,200 pounds of English-made mould candles: 1,000 pounds at four candles per pound, two lots totalling 700 pounds at six per pound, and 1,500 pounds at eight per pound. The candles were to be delivered at Fort William, and the whole supply was due by September 20. The notice records a tender, not a completed delivery.

A second Commissary's Office tender dated September 1 sought another 1,000 hogsheads of English or Spanish River coal for the King's Wharf and 2,000 pounds of English-made mould candles, comprising 1,800 pounds at four per pound and 200 pounds at six per pound. Proposals were due September 15. This was also a tender, not evidence of delivery.

A November 3 Commissary's Office notice sought English or Spanish River coal for delivery at the King's Wharf by September 1, 1809. The printed quantity reads `12,00 Hogsheads`; because the typeset number is incomplete or irregular, it is not normalized to a numerical quantity in the atlas.

## Public Hospital and civic works presentment

At the July 12 General Quarter Sessions, the St. John's grand jury described a Public Hospital near the town for sick and hurt fishermen, seamen and others as extremely desirable. The same presentment called for chimney inspection, removal of manure, rubbish and timber from public streets and paths, repair of a dangerous cut in the public road near the house formerly occupied by John Partridge, and additional burial ground. These were needs identified by the grand jury, not completed works.

## Coastal building restriction

Governor James Gambier's September 20 proclamation restated the instruction that no buildings could be erected within 200 yards of high-water mark except fishing stages, cook rooms, ships' rooms and structures necessary for curing and handling fish. It also prohibited taking land as private property, even beyond that distance, without permission. The sheriff and outport deputies were directed to remove unauthorized fences or structures and sell enough of the materials to cover the removal cost. The proclamation was reprinted on physical PDF page 1 of the April 20, 1809 *Royal Gazette* archive file.

## Fortifications and public works

Governor Holloway's September 29 report states that the St. John's fortification works had not advanced much during 1808 because the regiment was too weak to provide enough labour. The Royal Artificers had spent much of the summer preparing huts for winter quarters. Placentia fort remained as it had been the previous year because no dismantling instructions had arrived.

The same report states that the Harbour Grace Court House was complete, but Holloway deferred the authorized £200 payment until the district justices produced proper certificates. Bay Bulls residents reported that a jail was needed because deserters resorted there. Holloway proposed paying £200 to purchase and complete a house adapted for that purpose. The court house completion was reported; the Bay Bulls jail remained a proposal.

## Fishing Ships' Rooms register

Holloway reported that proclamations had initiated a survey and register of the Ancient Fishing Ships' Rooms in the respective districts. He singled out William Carter's active work, particularly in Ferryland, and recommended remuneration of at least £100. The report describes an active registration effort and a proposed payment. It does not establish that every district had been completed in 1808.

Holloway's September 22 proclamation provided the public review procedure. Claimants could inspect each district's Register of Surveys of Rooms at its Court House until September 29, 1809. After that date, an uncontested registered survey would be treated as the true survey and no other admitted.

## St. George's Bay coal report

Holloway reported coal found about twelve miles inland from the beach at St. George's Bay. Captain White of HMS *Avenger* had brought him a sample from the site, which he described as tolerably good. The letter establishes the reported location and sample; it does not establish a surveyed deposit, mine or production.

## Powder Magazine financing proposal

An October 17 grand jury presentment recommended an immediate subscription to build a Powder Magazine at Chain Rock. It also recommended limiting powder kept in any town house or store to 50 pounds, in packages, once the magazine was finished. The same presentment proposed fire rules for hay and straw, cooper and joiner shops, night work, chimneys, and carrying exposed fire between buildings. An Office of Ordnance notice in the same issue sought the return of six leather fire buckets marked `G.R.` that were lost during a recent fire.

A December 8 proposal then sought funds for a Powder Magazine serving the St. John's trade. The building property was to be divided into 50 shares of £10, a face value of £500. No person could take more than one share before May 1, after which remaining shares could be taken by subscribers. A proprietor-appointed committee would direct the building, and powder depositors would pay an agreed charge per package. The notice records the financing and operating proposal, not a completed magazine. It does not repeat Chain Rock as the location.

## Ordnance winter fuel tender

The Ordnance Barrack Office sought 150 cords of spruce or birch firewood for Fort Townshend, Signal Hill and the Ordnance Yard, in an agreed proportion at each location. The quantity was divided into three delivery instalments: one third by February 15, one third by March 15, and the remainder by May 15, 1809. The same notice sought 1,200 hogsheads of Sydney or English coal for the Ordnance Wharf by August 1, 1809. Proposals were due December 15 for firewood and December 20 for coal. The notice records a tender, not delivery.
