# 1824 System Record

## Electrical corrosion protection

The May 13 *Newfoundland Mercantile Journal* prints a report of Sir Humphry Davy's work on physical PDF p. 1. The report describes a weak chemical action between seawater and copper sheathing. It states that a small mass of tin in wire communication with a large copper plate made the copper negatively electrical and protected it from seawater. The Admiralty was moving the discovery into trials on warships.

This is evidence that an electrical engineering principle was published in Newfoundland in 1824. It is not evidence of a local installed electrical system.

## Fire alarm and engine response

The May 13 issue reports a St. John's fire on physical PDF p. 2. The fire alarm used guns and bells. Snow initially concealed the fire's location. The Fort Townshend engine arrived first, and Royal Artillery personnel checked the fire. Sergeant Major Gilmore's right hand was badly scorched. Responders wetted the adjoining stone house of Mr. Latour and prevented its roof from catching fire. The fire was confined to the house where it began and consumed that house in about one hour. The report did not establish the cause.

The issue date is Thursday, May 13. Its phrase "Saturday last" identifies the response as May 8, 1824.

## King's buoys

The May 13 issue prints a Naval Yard notice dated May 10 on physical PDF p. 2. John R. Mayne, Master Attendant, warned pilots and vessel masters that the King's buoys in the Narrows and St. John's harbour were intended only for warping past. They were not designed to bear a moored vessel. A vessel tied to one was to be cast off. A pilot who allowed the use was to be named publicly and barred from future King's service.

## Phoenix Fire Company

The May 20 issue prints the Phoenix Fire Company's formation and rules on physical PDF p. 3. Lower-district householders resolved on May 19 to form the company. A captain and three lieutenants led the company, and fire wardens assigned the other members to stations. Members had to obey officers, attend meetings and exercises, and drill with the engine from 18:00 to 19:00 every second Saturday.

When a fire alarm was given, members reported to the engine house at Brown, Hoyles and Morris's wharf. Each householder member kept six light buckets, shaped as inverted cones, ready for engine use. The rules imposed fines for missed drills and for failing to reach the engine house within ten minutes after the first alarm guns.

## Dart packet communication

The July 15 issue prints James Doyle's notice dated July 13 on physical PDF p. 3. Doyle had fitted the *Dart* packet for service between Portugal Cove and other Conception Bay ports. It left Portugal Cove at 08:00 every Saturday when wind and weather permitted. The notice names letter collection points in St. John's and Portugal Cove for commercial and other correspondence.

## Claim boundaries

The *Hibernia* packet notice repeated in several 1824 issues is dated September 18, 1823. It belongs to the 1823 record and is not dated as an 1824 service launch.

No supported 1824 Newfoundland lighthouse project, electric telegraph, installed electrical circuit or local lightning-damage event was found in the reviewed files. Foreign lightning reports and mechanical-engine reports were excluded from the public timeline.

## Newspaper search

The review covered all 25 surviving 1824 files listed in the MUN DAI *Newfoundland Mercantile Journal* collection, comprising 100 physical PDF pages. The MUN DAI main newspaper list and limited-run newspaper list identify no other digitized collection covering 1824. This collection boundary does not establish that no other 1824 newspaper existed or survives elsewhere.

Repeated notices were treated as continuity evidence, not separate operating events.
