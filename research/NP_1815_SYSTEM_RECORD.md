# 1815 System Record

## Fort fuel allocation

A Commissariat notice dated January 5 requested 150 cords of firewood for the St. John's garrison. It allocated 80 cords to Fort Townshend, 40 cords to Fort William and 30 cords to Signal Hill. The notice required sound wood delivered to the respective fuel yards by February 18.

## St. John's fire readiness

The February 2 *Royal Gazette* prints a Grand Jury presentment on the town's fire exposure. It called for every house to have one ladder from ground to eaves, a second ladder fixed to the roof and leading to the chimney, and at least two leather fire buckets marked with the householder's name. It also called for stove funnels to be protected where they passed through houses, stoves to be insulated from floors, and merchant gunpowder to be stored in the magazine. The Jury also identified boats, timber, dirt and wood obstructing public coves and streets.

## March fort-gun signal

Chief Justice Caesar Colclough's March 21 letter reports an alarming St. John's riot on what it calls "the night following the 16th." Colclough heard guns fired from Fort William and answered from Fort Townshend and Signal Hill. He described them as General Campbell's signals to have the troops ready if required. The letter states that the crowd dispersed because it believed the army was approaching. It also states that the signals called out magistrates and another civilian party.

## May security signaling arrangement

Colclough's May 6 letter reports that guards were doubled at different posts, Macbraire went to Fort Townshend to be ready to act with the soldiers, and patrols went around the Barrens every hour until daylight. He later told crowds at Sawyer's Corner that regular patrols would operate and that, on a signal, a company from each fort would come to town.

A postscript states that about 90 special constables had been sworn. It describes a nightly patrol of 24 people in two divisions for different parts of town, each division commanded by a Grand Juror, with a Justice attending the Court House nightly in rotation.

## King's Beach common sewer

A public-works notice dated August 29 sought proposals to construct and complete the common sewer required by the leases for the King's Beach ground. The notice states that the sewer was intended to carry waste from buildings on the leased ground and that the lessees had not taken steps toward fulfilling the agreement. The work remains a proposal in this record because the notice does not report completion.

## Survey and Record Office

Governor Richard Goodwin Keats's November 10 annual report states that a survey of lands near St. John's had been completed and lodged in the Record Office. He used the survey to identify encroachments and reported grants covering 843 acres.

The same report states that a new Record Office had been erected beside Government House under instructions dated March 22, 1814. It describes the building as secure and says the public records and papers would be lodged there when it was sufficiently dry.

## Source gap

Keats's November 10 covering letter says that he transmitted a report on the St. John's fortifications by the head of the Engineer Department. The attached report is absent from the reproduced volume. No 1815 fort condition claim is taken from the missing attachment.

## Newspaper and manuscript coverage

The review covered all 50 surviving 1815 files listed in the MUN DAI *Royal Gazette* collection, comprising 200 physical PDF pages. The files run from January 5 to December 28, with gaps on February 9, March 30 and several other weekly dates. The MUN DAI *Newfoundland Mercantile Journal* collection lists no 1815 files.

The official manuscript review covered all 144 physical pages of *Despatches from the Governor of Newfoundland to the Secretary of State for the Colonies, vol. 2, 1814-1815*. The volume combines both years. The 1815 pages include the March and May security reports and the November 10 annual report.
