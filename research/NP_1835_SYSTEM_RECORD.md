# 1835 System Record

## Cape Spear lighthouse

Chapter 6, dated May 8, states that the £1,000 authorized by the 1833 lighthouse act was insufficient to erect and complete the Cape Spear lighthouse and buy the apparatus required to light it. The act authorized a further loan of up to £500 at no more than six percent interest.

Annual principal instalments could not exceed £100. Principal repayment did not begin until two years after the debentures were issued. Interest was payable twice each year, on the last days of June and December.

The act documents additional financing and identifies the lighting apparatus as part of the incomplete project. It does not establish completion or operation in 1835.

## Harbour Grace Island lighthouse

Chapter 7, also dated May 8, established a lighthouse project on Harbour Grace Island in Conception Bay. The Governor in Council could appoint five commissioners to supervise and regulate its construction. The act authorized a loan of up to £1,000 at no more than six percent interest, with annual principal instalments capped at £200.

The commissioners had to submit the intended lighthouse plans, cost estimates, material and workmanship requirements, and contracts to the Governor in Council for approval. The Governor could issue warrants for lighting and maintenance expenses. After completion was certified by proclamation, merchant ships entering a port or harbour in Conception Bay were subject to a light duty of one penny halfpenny sterling per registered ton. Newfoundland vessels paid the duty once per year and qualifying fishing and coastal-trade vessels were exempt.

The *Royal Gazette* printed a commissioners' tender dated July 16. It requested specifications and tenders for the erection of a wooden lighthouse. Plans and further particulars were available from secretary William J. Henderson.

The statute establishes the project, financing, approvals and operating-fund mechanism. The tender establishes the specified structural material and the procurement stage. Neither source establishes completion in 1835.
