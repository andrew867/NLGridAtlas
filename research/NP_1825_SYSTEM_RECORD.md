# 1825 System Record

## Express packet communication

The November 3 *Newfoundland Mercantile Journal* prints an *Express* notice dated August 25 on physical PDF p. 1. The cutter had commenced packet service between Harbour Grace and Portugal Cove. It left Harbour Grace at 08:00 on Monday, Wednesday and Friday, and returned from Portugal Cove at 10:00 the next morning. Cabin passage cost 10 shillings, steerage passage cost 5 shillings, a letter cost 1 shilling, and parcels were charged in proportion. James Clift at St. John's and William Innott at Harbour Grace were the agents.

The same issue prints a second *Express* notice dated October 6 on physical PDF p. 3. The proprietors changed the connecting schedule so the packet could leave Portugal Cove early enough to reach Harbour Grace before night. The packet man was to leave James Clift's St. John's office at 07:45 every Tuesday, Thursday and Saturday. Letters and parcels were received at the office and regularly forwarded.

## Proposed Fanny coasting service

The November 3 issue prints Michael Hayes's notice on physical PDF p. 1. Hayes intended to employ the 51-ton schooner *Fanny* in the ensuing season in the carrying or coasting trade between St. John's, Harbour Grace, Carbonear, Brigus and other Conception Bay ports when freight offered. The notice invited freight and passage applications and allowed parcels and letters to be left at James Clift's wharf in St. John's.

The notice is a proposal. The five surviving 1825 issues reviewed for this record do not establish that the proposed service began.

## Claim boundaries

No supported named 1825 Newfoundland lighthouse project, electric telegraph, installed electrical circuit, operating electrical apparatus or local lightning-damage event was found in the reviewed files. A lightning-related Miramichi fire report and other foreign electrical references were excluded.

The November 3 issue also advertises repairs to King's Bridge. That road-work tender is outside this record's electrical, communication, navigation-aid and emergency-signalling scope.

## Newspaper search

The review covered all five surviving 1825 files listed in the MUN DAI *Newfoundland Mercantile Journal* collection, comprising 20 physical PDF pages. The collection contains issues dated November 3, 10, 17 and 24, and December 1. The MUN DAI main newspaper list and limited-run newspaper list identify no other digitized collection covering 1825. This collection boundary does not establish that no other 1825 newspaper existed or survives elsewhere.

Repeated advertisements were treated as continuity evidence, not separate operating events.
