# 1958 System Record

## Transmission

### Central Newfoundland

102L was constructed in 1958. The 2018 planning study divides the approximately 61 km route into 17 km from Rattling Brook to Notre Dame Junction, 20 km from Notre Dame Junction to Roycefield, and 24 km from Roycefield to Gander.

Source: [2019 Capital Budget Application, physical PDF pp. 174 to 175](http://www.pub.nl.ca/applications/2019/NP2019Capital/applications/NP%20-%202019%20Capital%20Budget%20Application%20-%202018-07-13.PDF#page=174), SHA-256 `c733684ac89212fe9fd1a2ac5f2198b6840692dd8f80260b5d08ae98471c5d5d`.

### Avalon Peninsula

- 41L was constructed between Carbonear and Heart's Content at 66 kV. The 2017 filing identifies 13.6 km of original line, 55 H-frame structures, 3 single poles and 4/0 ACSR. It excludes 2.0 km entering Carbonear that was built in 1974 and 5.3 km rebuilt after the March 2010 ice storm.
- 57L was constructed between Bay Roberts and Harbour Grace at 66 kV. The 2017 filing identifies 17.8 km and 186 H-frame structures of original construction with 4/0 ACSR. The 8 km section entering Upper Island Cove was built in 1989.
- 68L was constructed between Harbour Grace and Carbonear at 66 kV. The 2014 filing identifies 5.15 km of original line, 50 two-pole and three-pole H-frames and 4/0 ACSR. The 2.01 km section entering Carbonear was built in 1974.
- 15L was constructed between Molloy's Lane and Stamp's Lane at 66 kV. The 2015 filing records 4.22 km and 73 mainly single-pole structures, most with distribution underbuild. Twenty-seven original structures remained in the filed work area.

### Bonavista Peninsula and west coast

110L was constructed as a 79 km, single-wood-pole 66 kV line from Clarenville to Lockston.

403L was constructed as a radial supply to Robinsons and Flat Bay and connected Lookout Brook generation to the main grid. The 2004 filing records 11.6 km replaced in 2001 and a further 5.1 km selected for rebuild.

### 302L construction-year conflict

The 2002 Burin Peninsula Engineering Review says 302L was originally built in 1958 and partly rebuilt in 1969. The 2018 rebuild filing says the original Salt Pond to Laurentian line was built in 1959 and identifies a separate 2.4 km section entering Laurentian built in 1974. The construction year remains unresolved.

## Hydroelectric generation and civil works

### Rattling Brook

The plant entered service December 16, 1958 with two vertical Francis turbines rated 8,500 hp each and two generators rated 7,500 kVA each. Later records publish 11.5 MW and 14.8 MW plant capacities and a 1959 construction date; those differences remain unresolved.

Amy's canal was constructed in 1958 and the original Amy's Lake control gate was installed that year. The Rattling Lake stoplog spillway was commissioned in 1958. The filed structure had a 107.35 m overall length, 42 stoplog bays and 15 wooden stoplogs that raised its maximum storage elevation to 115.12 m.

### Lookout Brook

Lookout Brook G3, its Gilkes governor and the original G3 switchgear were installed in 1958. The 2010 filing records field-modified switchgear shared with G4, which was installed in 1984, and states that the G3 breaker could not be removed to provide visible isolation.

Source: [2010 Capital Budget Application, physical PDF pp. 184 and 186](http://www.pub.nl.ca/applications/ARCHIVE/2010/NP2010Capital/files/applic/NP2010ApplicationComplete.pdf#page=184), SHA-256 `e3e004d1aadd10be1624cc33b402203eb60c744f24e4736d1219b94e41579cb2`.

## Station equipment

The Mobile station record dates its 66 kV potential transformers to 1958. The 2008 filing proposed their replacement while retaining service through a bypass switch across the 24L breaker.

The oil-insulated cables supplying Stamp's Lane transformers SLA-T1 and SLA-T2 were installed in 1958. By 2002, their terminations leaked insulating compound and their metal bodies were cracked. The 2004 filing proposed replacement.

## Operations buildings

The Grand Falls-Windsor pre-engineered steel service building was constructed in 1958. A 28.0 by 10.2 m extension was added in 1988, and the building was converted to combined office and service use in 2007.

The approximately 1,200 m2 masonry and steel Stephenville building was constructed at Harmon Air Force Base in 1958. Newfoundland Power filings disagree on its purchase year, giving 1986 and 1988.
