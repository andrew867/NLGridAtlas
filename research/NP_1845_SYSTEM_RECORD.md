# 1845 System Record

## Operating telegraph described to St. John's readers

On February 22, the *Morning Post* printed a technical account of the Cooke and Wheatstone telegraph on the Great Western Railway. The article identified the Paddington to Slough route as 18 miles and described the revised apparatus as portable and connected by one pair of wires.

The article reported that the railway telegraph carried more than one hundred messages on some days. It said most messages concerned railway traffic and that the system assisted on days with heavy and extra trains.

The apparatus and operating line were in England. The record establishes technical information published in St. John's, not a Newfoundland installation.

## Communications baseline

The 1845 *Newfoundland Almanac* records the colony's mail connection before a local electric telegraph. It identifies the Royal Mail Steam Packet *North America* as the carrier between Halifax and St. John's, with calls at Arichat in both directions. The almanac gives an average three-day passage and a 72-hour stay at St. John's before the return trip.

The preceding page records steam-packet mail from Liverpool to Halifax and Boston. It prints 40 to 42 days for the average "course of the Post" between England and the colony.

No named 1845 Newfoundland telegraph line, company, station, instrument, construction project or operating circuit was located. The atlas timeline entry records the publication of technical information, not local infrastructure.
