# 1834 System Record

## Fort Amherst and Cape Spear

Chapter 4, dated June 12, placed the Fort Amherst and Cape Spear lighthouse works under five commissioners. It states that a building fit for a lighthouse and the required lighting apparatus already existed at Fort Amherst, at the entrance to St. John's harbour, but could not be used for lack of operating funds.

The act funded the system with vessel duties. A merchant ship entering St. John's paid one penny sterling per registered ton, no more than twice in a year. A coasting vessel paid an annual charge of ten shillings when above 60 tons and seven shillings and sixpence when below 60 tons.

The commissioners could borrow up to £1,000 at no more than six percent interest for Cape Spear. They had to obtain plans, cost estimates, material and workmanship requirements, and contracts for approval by the Governor in Council. Annual principal instalments could not exceed £200 and did not begin until two years after the debentures were issued.

The act also placed the public buoys, chains and moorings in St. John's harbour under the commissioners. Lighthouse funds could pay for their maintenance. The Governor could issue warrants for lighthouse lighting and maintenance, bells or cannon, boats and other appurtenances.

## Implementation records

A *Public Ledger* notice dated July 7 stated that a light would be exhibited from sunset to sunrise at Fort Amherst on the south head of the harbour entrance. This is an official service announcement, not a later operating log.

A second commissioners' notice, dated July 23, requested tenders to erect the Cape Spear lighthouse. The plan and specification were available from secretary John Shea. Tenders were due on August 2.

An October 6 notice requested pale seal oil and lamp wick for Fort Amherst for the following 12 months, delivered in quantities as required. It documents the lamp's fuel and consumable supply arrangement.
