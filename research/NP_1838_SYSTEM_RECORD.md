# 1838 System Record

## Transatlantic steam proposals

On September 25, the *Public Ledger* printed a letter arguing that Liverpool and New York steamers should call at St. John's. The writer proposed taking on coal, passengers and goods at the port. This was advocacy for a port call, not an operating route or government commitment.

Chapter 10, passed on October 25, authorized £500 per year for three years for a suitable steamer between St. John's and a port in Great Britain and Ireland. The vessel had to be at least equal to the steamship *Sirius* in steam power or burden. It had to make at least four outward and four homeward trips each year.

The appropriation established conditions for payment. It does not identify an operator, vessel or first voyage.

## Published interpretation and public notice

On October 30, the *Royal Gazette* asked correspondents in Newfoundland and England to publicize the bonus. Its article restated the statutory comparison with the *Sirius* as a vessel-size condition. It then expressed confidence that a suitable smaller vessel would receive the premium. The act itself specified steam power or burden, so the smaller-vessel statement is an editorial interpretation, not a statutory term.

A Secretary's Office notice dated November 5 and printed on November 6 made the steam-vessel item public for interested companies and individuals. It repeated the statutory payment, duration, route, *Sirius* comparison and annual trip requirement. The notice solicited an operator but did not name one.

The 1839 legislature recorded that the full £1,500 appropriation remained unappropriated. That later record confirms that the 1838 conditions did not produce a paid service under this measure.

## Telecommunications finding

The 1838 issues of the *Public Ledger*, *Newfoundlander*, *Royal Gazette*, and *Times and General Commercial Gazette* were searched for telegraph, electrical, galvanic, signalling, cable, wire, lighthouse and related terms. The located local signalling references concern military signals and ordinary ship signals. No named 1838 Newfoundland electric-telegraph circuit, Cape Spear telegraph station or commercial optical-telegraph service was located.
