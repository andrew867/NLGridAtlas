# 1810 System Record

## Town roads and bridges

A notice dated May 3 invited contracts to repair roads and bridges in St. John's. It directed contractors to obtain the work particulars from a four-person committee and submit proposals to that committee. The notice establishes a proposed repair contract, not completed work.

## Chapel and Powder Magazine

A notice dated May 31 requested accounts arising from the late enlargement of the Roman Catholic Chapel in St. John's. The wording establishes that an enlargement had taken place, but it gives no dimensions or completion date.

A June 7 notice invited proposals to rebuild the Powder Magazine. John Dunscomb, David Tasker, Newman W. Hoyles, Nicholas Gill and Samuel Bulley formed the committee receiving proposals. The notice does not establish completion.

## Ordnance materials and wharf

The Office of Ordnance invited a contract on August 23 to transport 1,000 tons of freestone from Cape Breton for His Majesty's works at St. John's. The contractor was to state a landing rate per ton; the stone contractors would place the stone alongside the transport vessel and assist with loading.

An Ordnance Barrack Office notice dated November 8 sought 150 cords of spruce or birch firewood for Fort Townshend, Signal Hill and the Ordnance Fuel Yard. One third was due by March 1, one third by April 1 and the remainder by May 1. Tenderers had to state a delivered rate per cord at each post, including cartage.

A November 15 notice invited contracts to extend and repair the Ordnance Wharf. A plan and description were available from the Commanding Royal Engineer in the Ordnance Yard. The notice required security and substantial, workmanlike performance; it does not establish that the contract was awarded or completed.

## Narrows signal and property register

A September 13 notice states that vessels had already been ordered not to pass through the Narrows without their distinguishing signal. It warned that the fort officer would fire at a vessel attempting passage by day or night, charge the vessel for the shots and prevent passage until payment.

An August 21 proclamation renewed property returns first ordered in 1804, 1805 and 1807. Surrogates, magistrates and sheriffs were to record the name and exact boundaries of each room or erection; harbour or cove; claimant; nature of claim; occupier; construction, sale or lease; date; transferee; consideration, rent and lease term. This is evidence of a structured property register, not a surviving complete survey.

## Land survey and Fishing Ships' Rooms

Governor Duckworth's November 25 report states that Sir Erasmus Gower had appointed Lionel Chancey to survey lands in the St. John's district. Chancey worked for a year but stopped because the work occupied too much of his time without remuneration. Duckworth proposed a completion reward and a permanent surveyor allowance of £25 per year.

The same report states that Duckworth made three perambulations of the Fishing Ships' Rooms in 1810 with the Chief Justice, the Vice Admiralty judge, magistrates and older inhabitants. Encroachments had accumulated to the point that recovering the real boundaries was considered nearly impossible.

## Hospital, churches, gaol and Fire Society

The governor reported that a St. John's committee had agreed to advance £700 toward a public hospital and wanted ground for the building. This was a proposal; the report does not establish construction in 1810.

The report describes Trinity church as ruined and in immediate need of repair, then proposes £80 in government aid. Greenspond inhabitants had subscribed £200 toward a place of worship estimated at £300 and sought the remaining £100. It states that an earlier government allowance for the Burin court house and gaol had not been drawn and that the gaol was expected to be complete the following year. It also records a proposal for matching assistance toward a church and gaol at Fortune Bay.

The St. John's Fire Society already existed. The governor reported that principal inhabitants had formed it, the Grand Jury had approved its rules, magistrates had sanctioned them and the rules had been entered in the Court of Sessions records. The Society sought current fire fines and fines from the previous three years, estimated at about £20, for its treasurer.

## Government House and Ships' Rooms plans

The governor described Government House as nearly uninhabitable and outside the military department's care. The Chief Engineer had examined it and prepared an estimate; a plan, section and elevation accompanied the report. Duckworth requested materials that could not be obtained locally so repair work could begin in spring. The record is a repair proposal, not evidence that the work was completed in 1810.

Duckworth also reported bringing plans of the St. John's Ships' Rooms to London and urged final regulation of the property. The record establishes the existence and official use of the plans, but not a completed 1810 cadastral settlement.
