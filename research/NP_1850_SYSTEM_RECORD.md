# 1850 System Record

## Halifax to Ireland proposal

On March 27, the *Morning Courier* reported that the United States Congress was considering a Halifax to western Ireland electric-telegraph cable. The reported route followed the Newfoundland Banks. The printed cost estimate was three to four million.

The article did not identify a Newfoundland land line, local company, survey, cable contract or construction work.

## St. John's station proposal

On November 20, the *Morning Courier* argued that St. John's should be promoted as the nearest American telegraph station to western Ireland. It printed Halifax at 2,155 miles and St. John's at a little more than 1,600 miles from western Ireland. The article called for evidence to be assembled and presented to governments and public companies.

The surviving November 20 scan says that a correspondent's communication follows, but it does not print the communication on any of its four physical pages.

## J.T.M. route proposal

The *Courier* reprinted the missing communication on April 1, 1854. The reprint identifies it as a November 20, 1850 item and reproduces a letter signed J.T.M. at St. John's on November 8.

J.T.M. proposed an overland line from St. John's to Cape Ray, passing near Holyrood and along the neck between Trinity and Placentia Bays. The letter proposed a 41 to 45 mile submarine section from Cape Ray to St. Paul's Island, with soundings described as 100 fathoms, and a little more than 12 miles from the island to Cape North. It claimed the route could save at least 48 hours between the Atlantic and the American continent.

The letter expected the Newfoundland government to encourage a British or American company. It did not propose that the government build, operate or maintain the line.

The route, distances, soundings and time saving are proposal values. No 1850 route survey, company, construction or operating service was found.
