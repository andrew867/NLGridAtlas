# Great Northern Peninsula reliability and Hawke's Bay backup generation

## 2021 to 2025 reliability record

Hydro's March 31, 2026 reply reports the following five-year averages on physical PDF p. 8.

| Measure | Great Northern Peninsula | Hydro customer average |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| SAIDI | 15.67 hours per customer per year | 17.94 hours per customer per year |
| SAIFI | 5.99 interruptions per customer per year | 5.61 interruptions per customer per year |

The same page assigns the region's outages as follows.

| Cause | Share |
| --- | ---: |
| Planned | 27% |
| Equipment | 24% |
| Adverse weather | 20% |
| Tree contact | 19% |
| Unplanned transmission outage | 10% |

The shares total 100%.

## System extent and design loads

Physical PDF p. 9 reports 16 terminal stations, approximately 700 km of transmission line and service to 13 communities. Its footnote 8 states that Northern Peninsula lines are designed for radial ice loads up to 1.5 inches and wind loads up to 110 miles per hour.

## Hawke's Bay diesel plant

The 2024 Annual Planning Assessment, physical PDF p. 42, identifies a 5 MW Hawke's Bay diesel plant that provides limited backup after loss of TL221. Physical PDF pp. 39 and 41 give Hawke's Bay T1 as 5/6.7 MVA, T2 as 2.5/3.3 MVA, a 6.34 MW transformer peak, and the typical use of a 15 MVA mobile transformer in winter.

The 2005 Capital Budget Application, physical PDF p. 102, identifies two 2.5 MW diesel units. It states that they provide standby power to Hawke's Bay, Port Saunders and Port au Choix, voltage support to the Great Northern Peninsula transmission system, and system reserve. The project replaced two original 1970 Allis-Chalmers air breakers with 5 kV, 1,200 A vacuum breakers.

System Operating Instruction T-063, revised August 31, 2011 and reproduced on physical PDF pp. 50 to 56 of CA-NLH-019, establishes the diesel plant's restoration role. When the Hawke's Bay units are required, both local feeders are opened before the terminal station is energized. The diesel is placed online before the feeders are closed. The same instruction uses Hawke's Bay or St. Anthony generation to control loading and support voltage during TL239 or TL259 outages.

Hydro's 2026 reply, physical PDF pp. 10 and 15 to 16, describes Hawke's Bay and St. Anthony as former principal sources that now provide backup power. It also reports a 2026 life-extension condition assessment for both plants.
