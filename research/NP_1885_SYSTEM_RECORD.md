# 1885 System Record

## St. John's Electric Light Company and Flavin Lane station

The St. John's Electric Light Company was formed in 1885 by A.M. Mackay. It built a generating station in the Terra Nova Bakery warehouse on Flavin Lane. A steam-driven generator produced 3,700 V direct current for arc lighting. A later reference describes eleven Water Street merchant display windows lit by 200 candlepower arc lamps.

## First station test and planned city lighting

The *Evening Mercury* reported on October 17 that the lamps at the Electric Light Company headquarters had been lit for the first time the previous evening. General lighting throughout the city was scheduled for that night. Several city shops had lamps, and the company expected to supply private houses with a milder light than the street lamps. The works were to be opened to visitors on the following Monday.

## Contact with telegraph and telephone circuits

The October 19 issue reported that the company's lamps were lit in several parts of the city on Saturday night. The light lasted only a short time because the new conductors contacted Telegraph and Telephone Company wires. Electric current entered the communications circuits. Operators were shocked, instruments were damaged, and the effect was reported as far as Holyrood.

At the Central Telephone Office, Tom Mallowney suffered burned fingers and was thrown back about two yards from the switchboard. The report states that 50 drops opened and sparks came from the batteries. The wires were repaired on October 19 and lighting was expected to resume that night.

The contemporary report does not identify the electric conductor voltage, contact location, pole number, protective equipment or repair method.
