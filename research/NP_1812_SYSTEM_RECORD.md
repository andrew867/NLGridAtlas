# 1812 System Record

## Fort fuel and construction material

A Commissariat notice dated January 16 sought 300 cords of firewood in equal proportions for the fuel yards at Signal Hill, Fort Townshend and Fort William. The listed delivery dates were February 20, April 20, June 20 and August 20.

An Ordnance notice dated January 23 sought 54 spruce posts, 60 spruce rails, 34 pieces of large spruce firewood and 1,500 spruce pickets for delivery at the Ordnance Lime Kiln on the Barrens. These notices record proposed supply, not completed delivery or construction.

## Government House fire

The issue filed as `TheRoyalGazette18120205.pdf` has a printed masthead date of February 6, 1812. It reports that a fire began at about 9 p.m. on “Monday evening last” in a servant's apartment in Government House at Fort Townshend. Troops, inhabitants and the established fire companies prevented the fire from spreading. The report says the roof of Government House and the barracks was preserved and the damage was inconsiderable.

The source does not print the fire's calendar date. The timeline therefore uses the publication date and retains the source's relative event wording.

## Windsor Road

The February 6 issue advertised a 600-ticket lottery at four dollars per ticket to improve the road from St. John's to Portugal Cove. A General Quarter Sessions notice dated January 31 required trees within 40 yards of each side of the road from Twigg's Mill Pond to Portugal Cove to remain as a screen from the weather.

## Garrison fuel, lighting and transport

An April 16 Commissariat notice sought 3,000 hogsheads of English or Sydney coal for King's Wharf, four specified lots of mould and dipped candles for Fort William, and 550 cords of firewood distributed among Signal Hill, Fort Townshend and Fort William.

A June 4 truckage notice requested separate prices for moving coal, firewood and provisions between King's Wharf and the three forts. It also listed firewood and provision movements between Fort William and Signal Hill. The proposed contract ran for 12 months from July 1.

An Ordnance notice dated May 14 and printed on June 4 sought 2,000 hogsheads of coal, 120 cords of firewood, two 40-foot spars, 100 tons of sand and 100 items printed as “Gumheads.” The source does not define that term, so it is retained without interpretation.

## Governor's Wharf

The July 30 issue published operating rules for the recently built Governor's Wharf in St. John's Harbour. The wharf was available for general town use. The rules established an application book, use in order of application, a wharfinger, removal of goods without delay and a daily boat charge. They prohibited wheeled carriages and heavy materials on the wooden part, vessels lying alongside, unattended boats and exclusive use beyond two successive days.

## Church fittings

A notice dated August 12 and printed August 20 announced that new pews in “the Church” would be sold by auction at the Court House on August 26. The notice does not name the church, so no congregation or building is assigned.

## Late-season Ordnance and fire-company records

An August 27 Commissariat notice sought mould candles for the garrison. An Ordnance notice on the same page sought 1,000 cubic feet of ton timber, 50 cords of straight firewood with spruce preferred, 30 gallons of seal oil and 1,000 rinds.

A Fire Companies notice dated September 24 asked persons with claims against the fire engines to submit their accounts to the company captains.

## Land and public works

An October 2 proclamation prohibited unauthorized land enclosure and building construction. It states that land had repeatedly been enclosed without grant or other legal authority.

A Commissariat notice dated October 15 sought boards, bricks, clapboards, laths, Roche lime, plank, building stone, timber and tar for public works. The quantities included 55,000 feet of boards, 22,000 bricks, 8,200 feet of clapboards, 800 bushels of Roche lime, 96 tons of building stone and 2,820 cubic feet of timber.

## Out-harbour defence

The governor's annual report dated November 1 states that principal out harbours were fortified after the war with the United States began. Ordnance, ammunition and small arms were sent from St. John's. Inhabitants laid gun platforms and raised breastworks at their own labour and expense. Engineer and artillery officers selected positions, and warships carried stores north and south. The report does not name the individual harbours.

## Fort William and Fort Townshend

The same report states that Fort William and Fort Townshend had recently undergone repairs. Their sea faces could command the harbour entrance. Platforms were renewed so stored guns could be mounted. The visually checked estimate is £360 3s. 7d.

## Churches, public wharves and ship-room plans

The annual report states that the Fortune Bay and Morton's Harbour churches were not complete and that Trinity church repairs were not complete. The approved sums had therefore not been advanced.

The report records two large public wharves erected for general accommodation. Two thirds of their construction cost had been paid. It records £20 paid to land surveyor Mr. Chancey and a £25 annual salary. The report says he had spent “the whole of the last Summer” making ship-room plans for leasing. Because the report is dated 1812, that relative phrase does not establish an 1812 survey date.

## Debtors' gaol proposal

The governor renewed a proposal for a St. John's debtors' gaol and High Sheriff's dwelling. A plan and estimate had been made. The estimate was £1,500, attributed to the high price of lumber. The existing gaol was reported unable to provide a proper place for more than one or two military prisoners. The record establishes a proposal and estimate, not construction.
